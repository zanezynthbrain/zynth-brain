## 1. Category Overview

Use when starting work in an unfamiliar industry or onboarding a new client.

### Structure:
**1. Market Definition**
- What is this category? How do customers define it (not how the industry defines it)?
- What is the approximate market size — Singapore and/or Myanmar?
- Is the market growing, mature, or declining? What's driving that?

**2. Key Players**
List 4–6 competitors. For each:
- Brand name + one-line descriptor
- Positioning (what they stand for)
- Estimated market presence (leader / challenger / niche)
- Primary marketing channels

**3. Category Conventions**
- What do almost all players in this category do the same way? (messaging, visuals, channels)
- What clichés or tropes are overused?
- What would be genuinely unexpected or different?

**4. Category Tensions**
The unresolved contradictions in how customers experience this market. These often become the most powerful strategic territory.
- Example: "Customers want personalised service but brands only offer standardised packages."
- Example: "People want to trust local brands but global brands dominate shelf presence."

**5. Strategic Opportunity**
A short paragraph (50–80 words) synthesising: given the market landscape, where is there a genuine gap that our client could own? Be specific and direct.

## 3. Audience Insight Report

Use when deep audience understanding is needed for a campaign or brand strategy.

### 3.1 Audience Segmentation
Define 2–4 segments. For each:
- **Segment name** (give them a memorable label)
- **Size / importance** to this client's business
- **Who they are**: demographics, location, role/life stage
- **What they want**: functional needs + emotional needs
- **What gets in their way**: barriers to purchase or engagement
- **What they trust**: review sites, influencers, media, word-of-mouth
- **Cultural context**: Singapore or Myanmar-specific nuance

### 3.2 Audience Tensions
The gap between what they say and what they actually do. These are gold for creative strategy.
- Example: "They say they want to support local brands — but they still default to international brands when quality is uncertain."

### 3.3 The One Insight
A single sharp insight that should drive the entire campaign. Format:
> **[Audience] [truth about their behaviour or belief] because [underlying reason] — which means [implication for the brand or campaign].**

Example: *"Yangon's young professionals follow international fashion trends avidly because status within their social circle matters more than price — which means our brand needs to signal aspiration before it signals value."*

## 4. Trend & Cultural Moment Scan

Use before campaign planning to identify relevant cultural hooks.

### Categories to scan:
1. **Macro trends** — Economic, social, or behavioural shifts affecting the market
2. **Category trends** — What's changing specifically in this industry
3. **Platform / media trends** — How content consumption is shifting (relevant to Singapore / Myanmar)
4. **Cultural moments** — Upcoming festivals, national events, tentpole occasions

### Singapore Cultural Calendar (Key Moments)
- **Jan–Feb**: Chinese New Year — family, reunion, prosperity themes
- **Mar–Apr**: Hari Raya Puasa — community, generosity, celebration
- **May**: Labour Day, Vesak Day
- **Aug**: National Day (9 Aug) — pride, unity, local identity
- **Oct–Nov**: Deepavali — light, new beginnings
- **Nov–Dec**: 11.11, 12.12, Christmas — promotional season

### Myanmar Cultural Calendar (Key Moments)
- **Apr**: Thingyan (Water Festival / New Year) — biggest celebration of the year
- **Jul**: Buddhist Lent begins — more conservative messaging period
- **Oct**: Buddhist Lent ends — festivals, weddings, spending increases
- **Nov–Dec**: Tazaungdaing Festival — light and celebration themes

## 5. Market Opportunity Brief

Use for new business pitches or entering a new market/category.

Structure:
1. **The Opportunity** — What gap exists and why now?
2. **The Audience** — Who is ready to buy, and what do they currently lack?
3. **The Competition** — Who occupies adjacent space, and why are they vulnerable?
4. **The Entry Point** — What is the most credible, low-risk way to enter?
5. **The Risk** — What could go wrong, and how do we mitigate it?
6. **The Ask** — What does ZYNTH recommend the client do next?

## 6. Research Quality Standards

### Source Hierarchy (use in order of reliability)
1. Primary data — client-provided sales data, customer interviews, surveys
2. Industry reports — Statista, Nielsen, Google Think, Meta Insights, local SG/Myanmar chambers
3. Platform data — Meta Audience Insights, Google Trends, LinkedIn Analytics
4. Reputable news — CNA, Straits Times, Frontier Myanmar, The Irrawaddy
5. Secondary/aggregated sources — use only to support, never as primary evidence

### How to Handle Data Gaps
Myanmar data is often sparse. When data is unavailable:
- State clearly what is known vs. estimated
- Use proxy markets (Thailand, Vietnam) with appropriate caveats
- Recommend primary research (client surveys, interviews) as next step
- Never fabricate data or present estimates as facts

---

## 7. Output Format Standards

All ZYNTH research outputs should:
- Open with a **"So What?" summary** — the 3 most important things the reader needs to know, in plain language
- Use evidence to support every claim — cite source or note it's an informed estimate
- Include a **"Strategic Implications"** section — what does this mean for ZYNTH's work?
- End with **"Questions This Research Raises"** — what would we want to know next?
- Avoid data dumps — every data point should serve a strategic argument
