## 1. Proposal / Scope of Work (SOW)

### Proposal Structure:

**Cover Page:**
- Client name + project title
- Prepared by: ZYNTH
- Date
- Version number (V1, V2 etc.)

---

**Section 1 — Understanding of Brief**
Restate the client's problem and goal in your own words — sharper and clearer than they said it. This shows you listened and thought about it.
- The business challenge
- The opportunity
- What success looks like

---

**Section 2 — Our Approach**
How ZYNTH will solve the problem. Not a list of deliverables yet — the strategic thinking behind the approach.
- Methodology / framework
- Why this approach is right for this client

---

**Section 3 — Scope of Work**
Itemised deliverables. Be specific — vague scopes cause disputes.

| Phase | Deliverable | Description | Rounds of Revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | | | |
| Phase 2 | | | |
| Phase 3 | | | |

**What's included:** List clearly.
**What's NOT included:** List explicitly — this protects ZYNTH from scope creep.

|---|---|

**Section 6 — Why ZYNTH**
3–4 sentences only. Not a full credentials deck — just the sharpest reason this client should trust ZYNTH with this project. Include one relevant proof point (case study reference, relevant experience, result).

Clear, numbered:

4. Project begins

## 2. Client Presentation Structure

### For strategy or campaign presentations:

**Slide 1 — Title**
Project name, client name, date, ZYNTH logo

**Slide 2 — Agenda**
What we're covering today. Sets expectations.

**Slide 3 — The Problem / Opportunity**
Restate the brief — sharper than the client said it. This slide shows you understood them.

**Slide 4 — Our Thinking**
The strategic insight or point of view that drives everything that follows.

**Slides 5–N — The Work / Recommendations**
Present one idea at a time. Each slide: one headline, one main visual or point.

**Second-to-last slide — Recommended Next Steps**
What ZYNTH recommends happens after this meeting. Specific, dated if possible.

**Last slide — Thank You + Contact**
ZYNTH details. Open for questions.

### Presentation delivery notes:
- Lead with the recommendation — don't make clients wait for the answer
- Every slide should have one clear takeaway in the headline
- Anticipate the top 3 questions clients will ask and prepare answers
- Always leave 10 minutes for questions — never run to the last second

## 5. Market Research & Competitor Analysis

When a client or the pitch team needs research.

### Competitor Analysis Framework:
For each competitor (3–5), cover:
- **Positioning:** What do they stand for?
- **Messaging:** What are they saying and to whom?
- **Channels:** Where are they active (social, paid, SEO, PR)?
- **Content style:** Tone, format, frequency
- **Strengths:** What are they doing well?
- **Weaknesses / Gaps:** Where are they vulnerable?
- **Recent activity:** Any new campaigns, launches, or moves?

### Synthesis output:
- What does every competitor do the same? (category conventions)
- What is nobody doing? (white space for the client)
- What should the client do differently based on this landscape?

### Research sources to use (Singapore & Myanmar):
**Singapore:**
- Company websites + social media (primary)
- LinkedIn company pages
- Meta Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library) — see live ads
- Google search — check organic presence and paid ads
- Straits Times, CNA, Marketing Interactive for industry news
- SimilarWeb for traffic estimates

**Myanmar:**
- Facebook pages (primary research channel)
- Meta Ad Library for Myanmar market
- Frontier Myanmar, The Irrawaddy for market context

## 7. Upsell Planning

Review accounts quarterly and identify growth opportunities.

### Upsell Triggers:
- Campaign delivered strong results → propose scaling or extending
- Client only using one service → identify adjacent need (e.g., doing social but no paid? Pitch paid.)
- Upcoming seasonal moment → proactive campaign proposal
- Client's business growing / new product launch → expanded scope
- Competitor making moves → flag and propose a response campaign

### Upsell Conversation Structure:
1. **Start with results:** What has ZYNTH delivered? Remind them of the value.
2. **Identify the gap:** What's the next problem or opportunity they have?
3. **Make the connection:** How does the proposed addition solve that?
4. **Quantify the opportunity:** What could they gain? (leads, reach, revenue)
5. **Make the ask:** Specific proposal, not a vague "we could also do..."

## 9. Handling Difficult Client Situations

### Scope creep:
"Thanks for sharing this — it sounds like a great addition to the project. This falls outside our current scope, so let me put together a quick cost and timeline estimate for you to review."
Never say yes to out-of-scope work without written approval and a change order.

### Missed deadline (ZYNTH's fault):
- Acknowledge immediately — don't wait for the client to chase
- Give a revised date you are certain of — not optimistic
- Explain what happened once, briefly — don't over-apologise or over-explain
- Deliver early if possible

### Unhappy client:
- Listen fully before responding
- Acknowledge their frustration without being defensive
- Separate emotion from the facts of the situation
- Propose a specific resolution with a timeline
- Follow up after resolution to confirm satisfaction

### Budget dispute:
- Always refer back to the signed SOW
- If there is genuine ambiguity, find a middle ground rather than winning the argument
- Document any changes to scope or fees in writing immediately
