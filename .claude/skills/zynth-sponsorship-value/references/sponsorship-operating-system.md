# ZYNTH Sponsorship Operating System

Read this reference for an event, campaign, content property, brand partnership, or owned IP that may be sponsor-funded or sell a sponsor package.

## A. Property Health Check

A sponsor package cannot repair a weak or undefined property. Complete this scorecard before tier design.

| Test | 1 — weak | 3 — developing | 5 — strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audience truth | Claimed only; no target/quality definition. | Target defined; limited evidence. | Verified or clearly forecasted audience, quality, context and consent boundary. |
| Distinctive promise | Generic event/content category. | Theme exists; weak reason to choose it. | Clear, memorable audience promise with a relevant moment/mechanism. |
| Rights/inventory | Logo list only. | Several assets, unclear scarcity/owner. | Scarce, high-value, deliverable rights and experience moments. |
| Commercial model | Revenue aspiration only. | Cost/revenue rough view. | Funding gap, margin, fulfilment cost, milestones, contingencies and owner visible. |
| Delivery readiness | No owner/plan. | Partial plan. | Delivery owner, schedule, venue/platform constraints, evidence capture and risks planned. |
| Measurement | Vanity metric only. | Some evidence possible. | Sponsor-objective-specific signal, source, consent and post-report plan. |

Do not issue an external rate card if any category is under 3/5. Repair the property or sell a discovery/strategic partnership conversation, not a promise.

## B. Sponsor Discovery Questions

Use these in a sponsor meeting or internal account research. Capture confirmed answer, evidence source and open question.

1. What commercial, brand, relationship, recruitment, product-trial, distribution, or community outcome matters in this period?
2. Which audience segment, account, community, geography or decision-maker matters most?
3. What has the sponsor tried before; what was missing or difficult to prove?
4. What category, competitor, brand-safety, calendar, budget-cycle or approval constraint applies?
5. Which assets can the sponsor genuinely activate: product, talent, expert, customer service, data, channel, venue, content or offer?
6. What would make the investment a success; what evidence would support that judgement?
7. Who decides, who signs, who activates, and what date/approval process determines the decision?

## C. Rights and Inventory Design

Build rights from the audience journey. Avoid long equal-weight benefit lists.

| Journey point | Audience need | Sponsor opportunity | Possible evidence | Delivery condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-awareness | Relevance / reason to attend | Content, invitation, utility, early access | Reach, RSVP, qualified registration | Audience permission and approved co-branding |
| Arrival | Orientation / anticipation | Welcome, service, product discovery | Queue/experience observation, scan | Venue access, staff, safety |
| Peak moment | Value / memory | Useful participation, stage/content integration, product proof | Attendance, interaction, content capture | Program, rights, production, no disruptive placement |
| Networking / conversion | Connection / action | Hosted meeting, demo, lead capture, expert session | Consented meeting/lead/report | Consent, CRM process, staff and follow-up owner |
| Follow-up | Continuity / proof | Recap, content, offer, community | Engagement, redemption, survey | Rights, data, channel plan |

## D. Tier Architecture

Use tiers only where they represent a different strategic role, scarcity, or experience—not a random addition of logos.

| Partner role | Typical job | Appropriate rights | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Title / strategic partner | Own a major business/cultural association. | Naming, category exclusivity, signature activation, senior visibility, content/data/proof package. | Require long lead time, high delivery cost, strong conflict/rights review. |
| Experience/platform partner | Make a journey moment better. | Named zone, useful service, interactive activation, content, opt-in follow-up. | Avoid disruptive activation or forced data capture. |
| Content/knowledge partner | Bring authority, expertise or format value. | Agenda/content integration, expert role, co-created assets, measurement. | Protect editorial integrity and speaker rights. |
| Hospitality/relationship partner | Reach target accounts or stakeholder group. | Curated hosting, meeting access, invitation/relationship protocol. | Never promise attendee access without permission. |
| Supporting/in-kind partner | Solve a delivery need. | Proportionate recognition, agreed product/service integration, fulfilment proof. | Value-match to cash equivalent and validate delivery/quality. |

## E. Indicative Commercial Model

Use an explicit model rather than invented price points.

| Input | Evidence / assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Total property cost | Approved budget/quote or internal planning figure, dated. |
| Fulfilment cost per right | Production, content, staffing, data, hospitality, venue/platform cost. |
| Funding target | Client contribution, ticket revenue, sponsor revenue or hybrid; timing and certainty. |
| Required margin / contingency | Apply current ZYNTH financial policy and state exception owner. |
| Scarcity and strategic value | Category exclusivity, access quality, activation depth, brand alignment, decision-maker relevance. |
| Comparable evidence | Prior verified sale, current market benchmark, quote or equivalent—not folklore. |
| Sales milestone | What must close by when for the property to proceed safely. |

Create conservative, expected and upside scenarios. Do not present upside as a forecast. An unclosed sponsor is not revenue.

Proof can include approved artwork, photo/video evidence, programme capture, delivery log, attendance/RSVP count, consented lead report, content links, survey evidence, and closeout acknowledgement. Capture proof during delivery; it cannot always be reconstructed later.

## G. Post-Property Sponsor Report

Use this structure. Report facts, not vanity language.

1. Sponsor objective and agreed rights.
2. What was delivered, with evidence and exceptions.
3. Audience/context evidence and its limitations.
4. Activation participation/content/relationship/lead outcomes, with consent/data caveats.
5. Qualitative signal: observations, quotes, feedback only when source is clear.
6. Commercial/result hypothesis: what the evidence may indicate; what cannot be concluded.
7. Recommendation: renew, change activation, retarget audience, improve proof or alter tier.
8. Closeout: outstanding deliverables, asset access, data/retention status and owner.
