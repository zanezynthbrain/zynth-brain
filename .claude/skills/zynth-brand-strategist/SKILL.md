## 1. Brand Foundation (Primary Output)

Use this for new clients or brand-from-scratch work. Always complete sections in order — each one feeds the next.

### 1.1 Client Discovery Questions
Before writing anything, ask or confirm:
- What does the business actually do, and who is the real buyer?
- What market are they in — Singapore, Myanmar, or both?
- Who are the top 2–3 competitors, and how do they position?
- What does the client believe makes them different?
- What is the business goal in 12–24 months?
- Is this a consumer brand (B2C) or business-facing (B2B)?

### 1.2 Brand Positioning Statement
Use this structure:
> **For** [target audience], **[Brand Name]** is the **[category]** that **[key benefit]** because **[reason to believe]**.

Rules:
- One sentence. No jargon. A 15-year-old should understand it.
- "Category" should be how the audience thinks, not how the industry thinks.
- "Reason to believe" must be provable — a process, credential, origin story, or proof point.

### 1.3 Brand Essence
A single phrase (3–5 words) that is the brand's north star. Not a tagline. Internal compass.
- Example: "Precision for the ambitious" / "Warmth that scales"
- Test: Does every product, campaign, and communication decision filter through this?

### 1.4 Brand Values
3–4 values maximum. Each must be:
- Active (a verb or behaviour, not a noun): "We challenge assumptions" not "Innovation"
- Differentiating: Would a competitor claim the exact same thing? If yes, sharpen it.
- Culturally relevant to SG/Myanmar context where applicable

### 1.5 Target Audience Profiles
Write 2–3 audience segments. For each:
- **Name & archetype** (e.g., "The Ambitious SME Owner")
- **Demographics**: Age, location, role
- **Psychographics**: Motivations, fears, aspirations
- **Media behaviour**: Where they discover brands, what they trust
- **Cultural note**: Any Singapore or Myanmar-specific nuance (e.g., family-first decisions, trust in authority, price sensitivity, dialect/language considerations)

### 1.6 Tone of Voice
Define 3 voice attributes. For each, write:
- **The attribute** (e.g., "Direct")
- **What it means for this brand** (1–2 sentences)
- **Sounds like**: One example sentence in-voice
- **Doesn't sound like**: One example sentence out-of-voice

### 1.7 Brand Story (Origin Narrative)
A short paragraph (100–150 words) telling why this brand exists. Structure:
1. The problem or tension in the world
2. Why the founder / team cared
3. What they built and how it's different
4. The future they're working toward

## 2. Brand Audit Framework

Use when an existing brand feels inconsistent, outdated, or is underperforming.

Audit across 5 dimensions (rate each 1–5 and explain):
1. **Clarity** — Is the positioning instantly understood?
2. **Consistency** — Does the brand look and sound the same everywhere?
3. **Differentiation** — Is there a real, defensible difference from competitors?
4. **Relevance** — Does it resonate with today's audience in SG/Myanmar?
5. **Emotional resonance** — Does it make people feel something?

Output: Score summary + top 3 priority fixes with rationale.

---

## 3. Competitive Positioning Map

When asked to map competitors or find white space:
1. Identify 2 axes that matter most to the audience (e.g., Price vs. Premium, Traditional vs. Modern)
2. Place 4–6 competitors on the map with a one-line descriptor each
3. Identify the white space — where no competitor lives
4. Recommend where ZYNTH's client should position and why

## 4. Market-Specific Guidance

### Singapore
- Audiences are multicultural (Chinese, Malay, Indian, Expat) — flag if a brand needs language/cultural localisation
- Trust signals matter: awards, media mentions, government associations carry weight
- Bilingual tone considerations for brands targeting older Chinese-speaking segments
- Price-value sensitivity is high even in premium segments — justify the premium clearly

### Myanmar
- Mobile-first audience — Facebook is the dominant discovery channel
- Trust is built through community endorsement and word-of-mouth, not credentials
- Burmese language copy consideration — flag when English-only will limit reach
- Emerging middle class in Yangon and Mandalay — aspirational messaging works well
- Sensitivity to political/social context — avoid anything that could be read as political

---

## 5. Output Format Standards

All ZYNTH brand strategy documents should:
- Open with a **1-paragraph executive summary** of the brand's strategic position
- Use clean headers (no emoji in formal deliverables)
- Include a **"Strategic Rationale"** note after each major decision explaining the *why*
- End with **"Next Steps"** — what should be briefed to Creative next

---

## 6. Quality Checks Before Delivering

Before presenting any brand strategy output, verify:
- [ ] Positioning statement passes the "15-year-old test" — plain and clear
- [ ] Brand values are active behaviours, not generic nouns
- [ ] Audience profiles include a cultural/market-specific insight
- [ ] Tone of voice has both "sounds like" and "doesn't sound like" examples
- [ ] Output ends with a clear Next Steps section for the Creative team
