# The ZYNTH Event Method — how a brief becomes a produceable, costed event

Run in order. Steps 1–5 are the thinking; the document is the write-up. Do not
start writing the plan until concept, journey, and run-of-show logic exist.

---

## Step 0 — Qualify the brief
You need: client + sector · **one primary objective** · guest count & profile ·
market (MM/SG) · date/venue constraints · budget band · funding intent (sponsor /
ticket / client). Two or more missing → ask sharp questions first.

**Objective → primary KPI:**
| Objective | Primary KPI |
|---|---|
| Product/brand launch | Reach + earned media + trial/pre-orders |
| Lead-gen / B2B | Qualified meetings / leads captured |
| Relationship / VIP | Attendance of target accounts + satisfaction |
| Awareness / community | Attendance + social reach + recall |
| Sales / activation | On-site sales / sign-ups |
| Celebration / internal | Attendance + engagement + sentiment |

## Step 1 — Strategic frame (why an event, why now)
State the business problem and why a *live* experience solves it better than media.
Output: the strategic proposition that anchors Section 2.

## Step 2 — Concept & theme (the creative)
Generate 3 concept territories, kill 2, defend 1. A concept must:
- Serve the objective and fit the guest profile.
- Be **ownable** — awkward for a competitor to copy.
- Carry a **signature moment** guests will feel and post.
Build the world so the reader can see it: theme, look & feel, colour, scenic
language, motif, naming, key visual, tagline. Output: Section 3.

## Step 3 — Experience journey
Map the guest arc: invite → arrival/registration → welcome → programme peaks →
networking/activation → close → follow-up. For each touchpoint: what the guest
sees/feels/does and the craft that lands it. Locate the **peak moment**.
Output: Section 4.

## Step 4 — Format & programme
- Choose the format (conference / gala / launch / roadshow / activation / hybrid)
  and justify it against objective + guest profile.
- Build the **programme table**: segment · duration · content · speaker/talent ·
  stage need. Sequence for energy (open strong, peak mid-late, close memorable).
Output: Section 5.

## Step 5 — Production design (make it real)
Translate the concept into build specs a vendor can quote:
- Stage/scenic, AV (LED/sound/lighting/livestream/power), décor/spatial (floor plan,
  zoning: registration / main / F&B / activation / green room), F&B format & menu,
  signage/wayfinding.
- Attach quantities and specs. Every creative idea → a production line.
Output: Section 6 (+ raw material for suppliers + budget).

## Step 6 — Run-of-show & operations (the day)
- Build the **minute-by-minute run-of-show** from crew call to load-out: time ·
  segment · action · owner · cue.
- Define **crew roles** (event director, stage manager, production manager,
  registration, hospitality, technical, security) with a one-line RACI each.
- Write the **operating SOPs**: registration flow, VIP handling, speaker management,
  comms protocol, incident escalation, contingency triggers.
- Build the **pre-event timeline** (week-by-week from kickoff to event day).
Output: Section 7.

## Step 7 — Sourcing & compliance
- List every vendor the plan needs (venue, AV/staging, lighting, F&B, décor,
  printing/signage, talent/MC, photo/video, security, transport, furniture, permits):
  category · vendor · deliverable · **contact** · indicative rate · lead time ·
  verified/indicative. **Never invent a contact** — unknown = "to source".
- Permits & safety (Section 9): required approvals + lead times for the market,
  crowd/fire/first-aid/insurance, weather plan, content-approval lead time.
Output: Sections 8 + 9.

## Step 8 — Cost it (bottom-up)
- Build the **itemised budget** from Steps 4–7 by category: qty × unit × total in
  local currency + USD at market FX, sub-totalled by workstream, with a **10%
  contingency** line and the ZYNTH **management fee (15–20%)**.
- Use `production-costs.md` bands; tag unverified lines `[INDICATIVE]`.
Output: Section 11.

## Step 9 — Funding model & margin
Using `sponsorship-and-commercial.md`:
- Compute **gross margin %**. Below the **35% floor** → fix it (re-scope, re-tier,
  re-price) before delivering.
- Decide **sponsor / ticket / client / hybrid** and justify from the sector's
  sponsorship dynamics + guest value. If sponsor-funded, build tiers + each sponsor's
  ROI; if ticketed, set price tiers + break-even attendance.
- State total investment, direct cost, fee, margin, payment terms (50% deposit).
Output: Section 12.

## Step 10 — Prove value (ROI)
Project the result vs the objective (leads / meetings / pipeline / attendance /
reach / brand lift), do the value math (pipeline value, cost-per-qualified-guest,
earned media value, break-even), and write the value narrative in the client's
language. Output: Section 13.

## Step 11 — Measure, de-risk, self-check
- Measurement (Section 14): metric · target · benchmark · tool · timing (pre/at/post).
- Risk register (Section 15): low attendance, weather, AV/power, no-show, permit
  delay, safety, over-run, supplier failure — each with mitigation + **on-the-day
  contingency**.
- Run the Critic rubric (bottom of `output-contract.md`). Fix anything below 8
  (and financial-law/operational-realism below 8) before delivering.

---

## Quick heuristics
- **Venue + F&B + AV usually = 55–70% of an event budget.** Get those three quoted
  first; everything else fits around them.
- **Book the peak moment first, then protect its budget.** The one thing guests
  remember is worth more than ten small touches.
- **Every run-of-show line needs an owner and a cue.** No owner = it won't happen.
- **Always carry a Plan B for weather, power, and a no-show speaker.**
- **Contingency is 10% minimum on production — live events bite back.**
