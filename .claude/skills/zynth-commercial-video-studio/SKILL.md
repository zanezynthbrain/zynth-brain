# ZYNTH Commercial Video Studio

## ZYNTH Operating Contract

Follow the shared [ZYNTH Capability System Standard](../../../docs/ZYNTH_CAPABILITY_SYSTEM_STANDARD.md). In particular: classify the work band; separate verified facts from assumptions; create three distinct territories for material creative work; make output executable and measurable; pass the relevant quality gate; and preserve founder/project-owner approval before external release, spend, client contact, vendor commitment, or publication.


## Mission

Create video that earns attention **because it expresses a specific human tension and brand role**, not because it uses a fashionable visual treatment. Carry one approved idea from brief to a finished, technically correct delivery plan, while distinguishing internal concept work, generated previews, real production, and released media.

A storyboard is not a production plan. A generated sample is not final footage. A beautiful edit is not effective without a distribution context, one message, rights/brand clearance, and an outcome measurement plan.

## Work Band and Release Rule

| Work | Skill may prepare | Human approval required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explore | Audience observation, narrative territories, script concepts, sample-frame prompts, storyboard drafts, edit references. | None for internal work. |
| Propose | Client-ready treatment, indicative package, shot plan, budget assumptions, make-versus-generate recommendation. | Founder/project owner before external presentation. |
| Execute | One approved preview or shot test in the approved production lane. | Approved project, scope, output count, and named production owner. |
| Release | Client delivery, publication, paid media, talent booking, music licence, location/vendor commitment. | Explicit human release/owner approval. |

## Preflight

Capture the following. If two or more critical inputs are unavailable, ask up to three sharp questions or make an internal hypothesis package with visible assumptions.

| Requirement | Questions to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Objective and action | What audience behavior, consideration, purchase, attendance, or perception must change? |
| Audience and placement | Who sees it, in what scroll/view context, format, device, language, funnel stage and market? |
| Brand truth | What brand kit, product facts, claims, mandatory message, proof, prohibited representation and competitor context are verified? |
| Scope | Duration, deliverables/cutdowns, deadline, budget range, production route, review rounds, owner. |
| Rights and safety | Talent/language/location/product/music/footage rights, minors, likenesses, cultural/religious content, regulated claims, data privacy. |
| Measurement | What signal will determine success; who owns it; what source and review date apply? |

## Core Method

1. **Write the communications problem.** State the audience, tension, outcome, message and desired action in one sentence. Do not start with camera style.
2. **Map the viewing behavior.** Identify the platform, attention window, sound-on/off expectation, language/subtitle requirement, format, context and CTA reality.
3. **Develop three narrative territories.** Each differs in the emotional/strategic mechanism—such as revelation, transformation, proof, participation, ritual, utility, contrast or character—not merely in music and lighting.
4. **Select the story engine.** Define the proposition, hook, narrative turn, proof, brand role, CTA, visual/sound grammar, and why this is credible for the audience.
5. **Create the treatment and script.** Use `templates/video-production-brief.md`. Write to time and platform, not to a word count. Clearly distinguish spoken VO, on-screen copy, visual action, SFX/music and legal/subtitle requirements.
6. **Build the production plan.** For every shot, decide: live action, existing rights-cleared footage, controlled AI preview, 3D/Blender, graphic/motion design, or combined. List the reason, risk, owner, asset source and dependency.
7. **Storyboard and previsualise.** Use frames to communicate direction; label them as concept/previs. Do not generate final logo, readable Burmese text, unverified product packaging, legal claim, celebrity/public likeness, child, or sensitive symbol in a model.
8. **Plan post-production before shooting/generating.** Define edit rhythm, captions, audio/voice, music rights, grade/look, motion design, master/cutdowns, review cycle, QC and release workflow.
9. **Score and hand off.** Use the quality gate. Route the work to the named director, producer, account/project owner and release approver.

## Narrative Territory Contract

Each territory must contain:

| Component | Required standard |
| --- | --- |
| Tension | A specific audience friction, desire, observation or belief barrier. |
| Proposition | One brand role/message; no collection of taglines. |
| Hook | What earns the next three seconds in the actual placement context. |
| Story engine | The change, reveal, proof, character journey, participation or visual mechanism that carries the idea. |
| Brand role | How the brand/product earns presence rather than appearing as an unrelated end card. |
| Craft world | Camera, performance, composition, art, light, pace, sound and motion logic—not a list of references. |
| Production route | Live/AI/3D/graphic decision, feasibility, rights, cost/timing risk. |
| Measurement | The desired signal and how it informs a later decision. |

## Production Route Rules

| Route | Appropriate use | Mandatory control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Live action** | Performance, authentic product/service behavior, sensitive/regulated message, complex interaction, real environment. | Approved treatment/script, call sheet/shot list, releases, location/talent/music/footage rights, safety and producer sign-off. |
| **AI image/video preview** | Internal proof of visual direction, abstract/non-identifiable situations, non-text product worlds, storyboard sample frames. | Approved project/scope, prompt and asset log, rights review, human QC. Never call it real footage or final unless verified/approved. |
| **Blender/3D** | Product/spatial/graphic camera control, buildable scene previsualisation, stylised visual effect. | Source scene, scale/asset/right notes, render plan, technical export check. |
| **Motion/typography** | Explain, compare, visualise data/process, create adaptable platform variants. | Verified copy and claims, editable source, type/language/accessibility QA. |
| **Existing footage** | Documentary proof or cost-efficient context. | Usage licence, source, expiry/territory/platform rights, brand and edit suitability. |

## Mandatory Output Contract

A complete video package includes the following, adjusted for scale but never replaced by a vague “creative concept.”

1. Executive overview: objective, audience, channel/context, core proposition, success signal, decision requested.
2. Three territories and recommendation: tension, hook, story engine, craft world, production implication and risk.
3. Approved treatment: narrative, visual language, sound/motion/tone, casting/talent/representation notes, product/brand integration.
4. Timed script: scene, visual action, VO/dialogue, on-screen copy, SFX/music, subtitle/translation, proof/claim source.
5. Shot/storyboard board: shot number, duration, purpose, composition/action, production route, assets/rights, notes.
6. Production plan: schedule, roles, make-versus-generate matrix, location/talent/crew/gear/AI/3D needs, cost assumptions and contingencies.
7. Post plan: edit structure, review milestones, grade, sound, music/licensing, motion/VFX, versioning, caption/subtitle and delivery masters.
8. Measurement and governance: KPI hypothesis, data source/owner/date, approval log, risks, assumptions, release restrictions and next owner.

## Video Quality Gate

Score 1–5; client-ready work requires 4+ in all mandatory applicable areas.

| Dimension | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Strategic clarity | The first three seconds, story and CTA advance a stated commercial objective. |
| Narrative and originality | A specific idea carries the piece; it is not a format trend with a brand pasted on. |
| Brand and representation | Verified brand/product/claim/language and respectful portrayal are controlled. |
| Craft and platform fit | Visual, sound, edit rhythm, aspect, captions, grade and motion suit the viewing context. |
| Production and rights | Every production route, person, asset, location, music and generated element has a stated control/assumption. |
| Technical delivery | Correct master/cutdown resolution, frame rate, audio target, captions, file naming and editable/source assets. |
| Measurement and release | Signal, owner, source, review date and explicit release decision are present. |

## Commercial and Governance Rules

- Budget, vendor rates, media performance and legal/regulatory guidance are indicative until verified by current source, quote or client/legal owner.
- Apply the ZYNTH margin and approval rules to any quoted package; do not book people, licences, locations, vendors or generation volume from this skill.
- Do not use or simulate a real person, client employee, child, celebrity/public figure, music, logo, product/packaging or location without a documented right/approval.
- Burmese/Myanmar and legal copy must be typeset from verified text after visual generation; subtitle review needs a named language owner.
- Keep a version trail. A client can only approve a specific named version, not an undefined “final.”
