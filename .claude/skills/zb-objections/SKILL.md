# ZYNTH Objection Intelligence

## ZYNTH Operating Contract

Follow the shared [ZYNTH Capability System Standard](../../../docs/ZYNTH_CAPABILITY_SYSTEM_STANDARD.md). In particular: classify the work band; separate verified facts from assumptions; create three distinct territories for material creative work; make output executable and measurable; pass the relevant quality gate; and preserve founder/project-owner approval before external release, spend, client contact, vendor commitment, or publication.


## Mission

An objection is decision data. Diagnose whether it represents lack of evidence, poor fit, risk, timing, scope, budget architecture, internal politics, or a genuine “no.” The goal is a clearer next decision—not a clever rebuttal.

## Input Contract

Capture the exact objection/quote, speaker role, account objective, proposal/service, decision stage, supplied evidence, commercial constraints, prior attempts, unknowns, and desired next decision. Do not write a response from a generic objection label alone.

## Method

1. **Name the stated concern.** Preserve the buyer’s actual language; do not convert “we need to think” into a false price objection.
2. **Diagnose the underlying risk.** Classify as value, trust/proof, budget/cash flow, scope, timing, capability, internal approval, procurement/legal, priority, or mismatch.
3. **Assess legitimacy.** Determine whether ZYNTH can answer with evidence, restructure the offer, run a discovery/test, defer, or should decline.
4. **Build the response.** Use the sequence: acknowledge → clarify → evidence/constraint → tailored option → honest trade-off → next decision. Never claim certainty where only a hypothesis exists.
5. **Prepare proof and boundaries.** Identify the case/artefact/metric/role that supports the answer, what remains an assumption, and what ZYNTH will not promise or discount without approval.
6. **Set the next step.** Specify the decision owner, question, artifact, date, and whether founder/commercial approval is required.

## Response Architecture

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Acknowledge | Demonstrate accurate understanding without conceding an untrue premise. |
| Clarify | Ask one question that distinguishes the stated concern from the underlying decision criterion. |
| Evidence | Use a verified case, method, scope, measurement plan, quote, timeline, or honest limitation. |
| Option | Offer a fit-for-purpose decision path, not an automatic discount. |
| Trade-off | State what changes if scope/time/cost/risk changes. |
| Ask | Secure a specific next decision, discovery input or stakeholder conversation. |

## Objection Playbooks

| Objection type | Diagnose | Appropriate response route | Never do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| “Too expensive” | Value, cash flow, comparator, scope, approval or priority? | Reconnect to outcome/value, show scope and cost drivers, offer phased/gated route if viable, obtain commercial approval for any change. | Defend price emotionally, discount immediately, conceal reduced scope. |
| “Can you guarantee results?” | Measurement maturity, risk tolerance, past disappointment? | Explain controllable inputs, test/measurement plan, outcome hypothesis, and limits of attribution. | Promise revenue/ROAS/attendance/viral reach. |
| “We can do this in-house” | Actual capability, capacity, speed, independence or political concern? | Identify the missing capability/peak load/independent perspective; offer a defined specialist role if genuine. | Attack the internal team or sell work they do not need. |
| “We already have an agency” | Incumbent strength, gap, contract, relationship, timing? | Respect the relationship; identify a narrow complementary problem only if evidenced. | Demand replacement or imply incumbent failure. |
| “Not now” | Timing, budget, calendar, no urgency or polite rejection? | Identify trigger/date; offer a useful future checkpoint or close respectfully. | Fake scarcity or endless follow-up. |
| “We need more proof” | Trust, fit, risk, stakeholder confidence? | Share verified relevant method/case, scoped diagnostic or discovery workshop, transparent assumptions. | Fabricate case results or overstate similarity. |
| “Send a proposal” | Real opportunity or information collection? | Qualify objective, stakeholders, scope, decision process and next meeting before bespoke effort. | Produce a full free solution with no discovery/approval. |

## Output Contract

Use `templates/objection-response-plan.md`. The output contains the exact concern, diagnosis, evidence state, response, commercial/approval boundary, discovery question, next decision, owner, date and CRM/project note.

## Quality and Governance

A response passes only when it is specific to the account, truthful about proof and limitations, commercially viable, respectful, and leads to a defined next decision. The founder/authorised commercial owner approves discounts, exceptions, contractual terms, performance commitments and external communications.

Read `references/objection-diagnosis.md` for detailed prompts and decision trees.
