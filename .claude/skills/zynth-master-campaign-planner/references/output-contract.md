# The ZYNTH Master Plan Standard — Output Contract

Every campaign plan contains ALL of these sections, in order, fully populated.
Skipping one is a stated decision, never a silent gap. Use real tables (not prose
lists) for anything with numbers. Target length: a complete plan, typically
6,000–15,000+ words depending on scale.

---

## 0. Cover & one-line ask
- Client, sector, campaign name, market, prepared-by (ZYNTH), date.
- **The one-line ask:** one sentence — *who engages/​sponsors ZYNTH to do what, for
  what total investment, targeting which single headline result.* The client
  should be able to say yes from this line alone.

## 1. Executive Summary (1 page)
- The opportunity in the client's own market terms.
- The proposed idea in 2–3 sentences.
- The headline outcome (the result) and the investment.
- Why ZYNTH. Written so a busy decision-maker can approve from this page.

## 2. Situation & Market Insight
- What is happening in the client's category right now (sector-grounded — pull from
  the sector pack).
- The competitive landscape: 3–5 named competitors, what they do, and the gap.
- The single sharp **audience insight** the campaign is built on (a truth about
  what the audience believes/wants that the brand can own).

## 3. Target Audience (in detail)
- Primary + secondary segments: demographics, psychographics, behaviours, pain
  points, and **media consumption** (which platforms, when, in what language).
- A short "day in the life" / decision journey for the core segment.
- What will actually move them (message + proof they need).

## 4. The Big Idea & Creative Direction
- **The idea in one line**, then the expansion. Distinctive and defensible — not a
  list of tactics wearing a name.
- **Concept rationale:** why THIS idea and not the obvious one; the territories
  considered and rejected; why it fits this brand and no other.
- **Creative direction:** the visual world (art direction, colour, typography,
  imagery, mood), the tone of voice, key art / hero-visual direction, and 2–3
  reference/mood cues. The reader should be able to *see and feel* the campaign.
- **Key message + tagline** (bilingual where useful).

## 5. Campaign Architecture (phase by phase)
- The full journey: **Awareness → Consideration → Conversion → Advocacy** (or, for
  launches: Tease → Launch → Sustain → Amplify). One row per phase with: objective,
  channels, content, and the KPI it drives.
- How each phase hands to the next; the "peak moment" that makes it memorable.

## 6. Channel Strategy & Budget Split
- Table: channel · role · % of budget · why this channel for this audience.
- Grounded in the sector's real platform mix (e.g. MM = Facebook-first + Viber; SG =
  multi-platform + LinkedIn for B2B). Use the budget-allocation benchmarks by
  objective (awareness/lead-gen/sales) from the commercial-model reference.

## 7. Content Plan
- Content pillars (3–5) and content types per channel.
- A **detailed 2–4 week content calendar** table: date · channel · content · format
  · owner · goal.
- Posting cadence and the first-3-seconds hook logic for short-form.

## 8. Design Concepts
- The visual system applied: key-visual direction, layout/templating approach,
  motion/animation direction, and how the brand shows up across formats.
- Specific format specs (feed 1080×1350, Reels/TikTok 1080×1920, etc.).

## 9. Paid Media / Ads Management Plan
- Per platform: objective, audience targeting (interests/lookalike/retargeting),
  budget, flighting, and **expected results** (impressions, reach, CPC/CPM,
  clicks, conversions) using real market CPM/CPC bands.
- The test-and-scale method: what's A/B tested, the optimisation triggers, and the
  reporting cadence.

## 10. Influencer / KOL Strategy (if applicable)
- Tier selection (nano/micro/mid/top) with rationale, named-type creators for the
  sector, collaboration format, deliverables, and expected reach/engagement.

## 11. Execution Plan & Workflow (how it's managed and driven)
- **Week-by-week timeline** table with milestones from kickoff to wrap.
- **Workstreams + owners** (strategy, creative, production, media, reporting).
- The **SOP / operating rhythm**: approval gates (ZYNTH drafts → client approves),
  review cadence, and how the campaign is steered week to week.
- What ZYNTH provides vs what the client provides vs what is outsourced.

## 12. Suppliers, Vendors & Production
- Table: category · recommended vendor(s) · what they deliver · **contact** ·
  indicative rate · lead time · verified/indicative. Pull from ZYNTH's supplier DB
  and the sector pack; never invent a contact — mark unknowns "to source".
- Production notes: shoot/asset requirements, permits, censorship/regulatory lead
  times (e.g. 2025 Motion Picture Law for video), font rule (Unicode/Pyidaungsu).

## 13. Detailed Budget (itemised)
- Full line-item table: item/activity · description · qty · unit cost · total
  (local currency + USD) · % of total. Sub-totalled by workstream.
- Market FX stated. 10% contingency line. Nothing rounded to hide assumptions.

## 14. Investment, ZYNTH Profit & Commercial Model
- **Total client investment** (or total sponsorship target).
- Direct cost vs ZYNTH management fee (15–20%) vs margin — shown transparently.
- **Gross margin %** (must clear the 35% floor; target 40%).
- **The model decision:** client-funded (sell the campaign) OR sponsor-funded (sell
  sponsors a tiered package) — with the rationale (see commercial-model reference).
  If sponsor-funded, include the sponsor tiers, what each tier gets, and the
  sponsor's ROI.
- Payment terms (50% deposit / balance schedule).

## 15. ROI Projection, Results & Client Value
- **The result the client gets:** projected outcomes vs the objective (reach,
  leads, sales, sign-ups, brand lift) with the assumptions shown.
- Break-even / value math: what the outcome is worth to the client vs the
  investment. Make the benefit unmistakable and quantified.
- **Why it's worth it** — the value narrative in the client's language.

## 16. Measurement & KPIs
- Table: metric · target · benchmark · measurement tool · reporting frequency.
- Tied back to the Section 1 objective. Include the dashboard/reporting cadence.

## 17. Risk Register & Mitigation
- Table: risk · likelihood · impact · mitigation. Cover platform/policy, cultural/
  religious timing, production, weather (for on-ground), and low-performance triggers.

## 18. Terms & Why ZYNTH
- Payment, cancellation, scope-change, IP, force majeure (standard).
- Client responsibilities (approvals, assets, access).
- Short, credible "Why ZYNTH" — speed (48h), craft, dual-market, real market pricing.

## Self-check — the Critic rubric (score before delivering)
Score 1–10 each; do not deliver below 8, or below 8 on financial-law:
- **Commercial logic** — does the money and the model make sense?
- **Market grounding** — real MM/SG numbers, sector-specific, market FX?
- **Concept distinctiveness** — could this only be this brand?
- **Completeness** — every section full, real tables, no placeholders?
- **Financial-law compliance** — 35% floor held, 50% deposit, verified-vs-indicative tagged?
- **Value clarity** — is the client's result and benefit unmistakable?
