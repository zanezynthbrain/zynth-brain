# The ZYNTH Planning Method — how a brief becomes a costed, winning plan

This is the step-by-step IP. Run it in order. Each step produces the raw material
for one or more sections of `output-contract.md`. Do not skip to writing the plan
until Steps 1–4 are done in your head (or on scratch) — that is where the thinking
lives; the document is just the write-up.

---

## Step 0 — Qualify the brief (before anything)
You need six things. If two or more are missing, ask sharp questions first.
- **Client + sector** (→ load the sector pack in `sectors.md`).
- **Objective** — pick ONE primary: awareness, lead-gen, sales, launch, loyalty,
  or reputation. Everything downstream serves it. A plan with five objectives has none.
- **Budget band** (or "we set it") and market (MM / SG / both).
- **Timing / occasion** — festival, launch date, fiscal quarter, always-on.
- **Audience** — who must act, and what "success" looks like to the client.
- **Constraints** — regulatory (banking, pharma, alcohol, tobacco), brand rules,
  no-go competitors, censorship lead times.

**Objective → primary KPI map** (lock this now):
| Objective | Primary KPI | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach / impressions / recall lift | Video views, share of voice |
| Lead-gen | Qualified leads / CPL | Form completion rate, MQL→SQL |
| Sales | Revenue / ROAS / units | CAC, conversion rate |
| Launch | Trial / sign-ups / footfall | Awareness in target segment |
| Loyalty | Retention / repeat rate | NPS, referral rate |
| Reputation | Sentiment / earned reach | Media pickups, brand favourability |

## Step 1 — Strategic diagnosis (the "why now")
Frame the real business problem, not the ask. Use whichever lens fits:
- **The 1-2-3:** one business problem → two audience truths → three ways in.
- **Barrier vs. driver:** what stops the audience acting, what would move them.
- **Category convention → break:** what everyone in the sector does (from the sector
  pack's "creative angles" + competitor scan), and the tension ZYNTH can exploit.
Output: a single **strategic proposition** — "To [objective], we must get [audience]
to [belief/action] by [the lever]." This becomes Section 2.

## Step 2 — Audience decoding
From the sector pack + the brief, build the core segment:
- Demographics + psychographics + **media day** (which platform, when, what language).
- The **buying/decision journey** and where the drop-off is.
- The **one insight** — a true, specific, slightly uncomfortable thing the audience
  believes/wants that the brand can credibly own. Not a fact; a tension.
Output: Section 3 + the insight that anchors the Big Idea.

## Step 3 — The Big Idea (creative direction)
Generate 3 territories, kill 2, defend 1. A territory must:
- Spring from the insight (Step 2), not from a format.
- Be **ownable** by this brand and awkward for a competitor to copy.
- Extend across the whole funnel (it must work as a teaser AND as a retail POSM).
Then build the direction so the reader can *see* it:
- **Idea line** → expansion → **why this and not the obvious one** (name the rejected
  routes).
- **Visual world:** art direction, colour, type, imagery, motion, 2–3 mood cues.
- **Voice + key message + tagline** (bilingual where it earns its place).
Output: Sections 4, 8 (design), and the spine of 5/7.

## Step 4 — Architecture & channel logic
- Lay the idea across phases: **Awareness → Consideration → Conversion → Advocacy**
  (or Tease → Launch → Sustain → Amplify for launches). Each phase gets an objective,
  the channels, the content, and the KPI it moves.
- Choose channels from the sector's real mix, then split budget by the
  **objective-based allocation** in `commercial-model.md` (don't spread evenly —
  weight to the primary KPI).
- Define the **peak moment** — the one beat (a stunt, a film, an event, a drop) the
  whole campaign is built to land.
Output: Sections 5 and 6.

## Step 5 — Content & always-on
- 3–5 **content pillars** tied to the idea; content types per channel.
- A **2–4 week calendar** (date · channel · content · format · owner · goal).
- Short-form hook logic (first 3 seconds), cadence, and the community-management SOP
  (response time, tone, escalation).
Output: Section 7.

## Step 6 — Paid media modelling
For each paid platform:
- Objective, targeting (interest / lookalike / retargeting), budget, flighting.
- **Forecast results** from real market CPM/CPC bands (see `commercial-model.md`):
  impressions = budget ÷ CPM × 1000; clicks = impressions × CTR; conversions =
  clicks × CVR. State every assumption.
- Test-and-scale: what's A/B tested, the win condition, the optimisation trigger,
  reporting cadence.
Output: Section 9 (+ feeds the ROI model).

## Step 7 — Execution design (make it run smoothly)
This is what turns a deck into a delivered campaign.
- **Week-by-week timeline** from kickoff to wrap, with milestones and dependencies.
- **Workstreams + owners:** strategy, creative, production, media, reporting —
  who does what, and the RACI in one line each.
- **Operating rhythm / SOP:** the approval gates (ZYNTH drafts → client approves in
  ≤X days), the weekly review, the change-request path, the escalation ladder.
- **Make/buy split:** what ZYNTH does in-house vs what is outsourced (→ suppliers).
Output: Section 11.

## Step 8 — Sourcing & production
- From the sector pack + ZYNTH supplier DB, list every vendor the plan needs:
  category · vendor · deliverable · **contact** · indicative rate · lead time ·
  verified/indicative. **Never invent a contact** — unknown = "to source".
- Note production realities: shoot/asset list, permits, censorship/regulatory lead
  times (e.g. 2025 Motion Picture Law), font rule (Myanmar Unicode/Pyidaungsu).
Output: Section 12 (+ real numbers for the budget).

## Step 9 — Cost the whole thing (bottom-up)
- Build the **itemised budget** from Steps 4–8: every line has qty × unit × total
  in local currency + USD at market FX, sub-totalled by workstream, with a **10%
  contingency** line on production.
- Add the **ZYNTH management fee (15–20% of spend)**.
- This is the truth layer — the creative promises nothing the budget can't fund.
Output: Section 13.

## Step 10 — Commercial model & margin (the ZYNTH decision)
Using `commercial-model.md`:
- Compute **gross margin %**. If it's below the **35% floor**, fix it (re-scope,
  re-price, or change the model) — do not deliver a floor-breaking plan silently.
- Decide **sell vs sponsor:** does the client fund this directly, or is it
  sponsor-funded (ZYNTH sells tiered sponsor packages)? Justify with the sector's
  sponsorship dynamics. If sponsor-funded, build the tiers + each tier's ROI.
- State total investment, direct cost, fee, margin, and payment terms (50% deposit).
Output: Section 14.

## Step 11 — Prove the value (ROI)
- Project the **result** against the Step 0 objective (reach / leads / sales /
  sign-ups / brand lift) with assumptions visible.
- Do the **value math:** what the outcome is worth to the client vs the investment —
  break-even, ROAS, or cost-per-outcome. Make the benefit quantified and obvious.
- Write the **value narrative** in the client's language (Section 15).

## Step 12 — De-risk, measure, self-check
- **Risk register** (Section 17): platform/policy, cultural/religious timing,
  production, weather, low-performance triggers — each with a mitigation.
- **Measurement** (Section 16): metric · target · benchmark · tool · frequency.
- **Run the Critic rubric** (bottom of `output-contract.md`). Score commercial
  logic, market grounding, concept distinctiveness, completeness, financial-law,
  value clarity. Anything below 8 (or below 8 on financial-law) gets fixed before
  the plan leaves your hands.

## Quick heuristics
- **Weight to the objective.** 60–70% of budget to the primary KPI's channels.
- **One peak beat** beats five clever tactics. Fund the beat.
- **Every creative claim needs a budget line and a supplier.** If you can't cost it
  or source it, it's a wish, not a plan.
- **Bilingual where it converts**, not everywhere — MM mass = Burmese-first; SG B2B
  = English + LinkedIn.
- **If the floor won't hold, say so on the page** and propose the path to 40%.
