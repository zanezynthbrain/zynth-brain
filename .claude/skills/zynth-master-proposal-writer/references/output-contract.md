# The Anatomy of a Winning ZYNTH Proposal — Output Contract

Every proposal contains ALL of these, in order, fully populated. This is the structure
that wins: it moves the client from *"they get us"* → *"that's a great idea"* → *"it will
work"* → *"it's worth it"* → *"yes."* Use real tables for anything with numbers.

---

## 0. Cover & the one-line ask
Client, sector, proposal title (name the idea), market, prepared-by (ZYNTH), date, and
**the one-line ask**: *who engages/sponsors ZYNTH to do what, for what investment,
targeting which single headline result.* Approvable from this line alone.

## 1. Executive Summary (1 page — written last, read first)
Their opportunity in their terms · the idea in 2–3 sentences · the headline result and
the investment · why ZYNTH. A busy decision-maker can say yes from this page.

## 2. We Understand Your Challenge (client-first)
Their business problem and world, in THEIR words — proving ZYNTH gets it before selling.
The market moment, the stakes, and the specific thing they need to move. This section
earns the right to be heard.

## 3. The Insight & The Opportunity
The single sharp audience/market insight the whole proposal turns on (a true, ownable
tension), plus the competitive gap (3–5 named rivals, what they do, where the opening is).

## 4. The Big Idea & Creative Direction
The idea in one line, then the expansion · why THIS idea and not the obvious one
(territories considered/rejected) · the visual/tonal world so the reader can see and feel
it · key message + tagline (bilingual where useful). Distinctive and defensible.

## 5. How We'll Execute (the plan made concrete)
Exactly how it runs: phases/architecture, channels, content, production, and the peak
moment. For events: the programme + run-of-show logic. The reader sees the machine, not
a promise.

## 6. Workstreams, Timeline & Ways of Working
Week-by-week timeline with milestones · workstreams + owners · the operating rhythm
(ZYNTH drafts → client approves in ≤X days) · what ZYNTH does vs client vs outsourced.

## 7. Suppliers & Production
Table: category · vendor(s) · deliverable · contact · indicative rate · lead time ·
verified/indicative. Never invent a contact — unknown = "to source". Production realities
(permits, censorship/regulatory lead times, font rule).

## 8. Investment — Itemised Budget
Full line-item table: item · description · qty · unit cost · total (local + USD at market
FX) · % of total, sub-totalled by workstream. 10% contingency line. Nothing rounded to
hide assumptions. Then the **headline investment figure**, clearly.

## 9. Commercial Model & Value to ZYNTH
Direct cost vs 15–20% management fee vs margin (transparent) · gross margin % (clears the
35% floor, targets 40%) · the model decision — **sell the campaign/event OR sponsor-fund
it** (with rationale; if sponsor-funded, the tiers + each sponsor's ROI) · payment terms
(50% deposit / balance schedule).

## 10. The Results You'll Get (ROI & value)
Projected outcomes vs the objective (reach/leads/sales/sign-ups/attendance/brand lift)
with assumptions shown · the value math (what the outcome is worth vs the investment —
break-even, ROAS, cost-per-outcome) · **the single headline benefit number** · the value
narrative in the client's language. This is where the proposal is won or lost — make the
benefit unmistakable.

## 11. Measurement & KPIs
Table: metric · target · benchmark · tool · reporting cadence. Tied to the Section 1
objective, with the dashboard/reporting rhythm.

## 12. Why ZYNTH (proof & credibility)
Relevant thinking and method, the dual-market edge (MM + SG), speed (48h proposals),
craft, and real market pricing. Credible and specific — never fabricated clients or cases.

## 13. Risk & Mitigation
Table: risk · likelihood · impact · mitigation. Platform/policy, cultural/religious
timing, production, weather (on-ground), and low-performance triggers.

## 14. Terms & Next Step (the close)
Payment, scope-change, IP, cancellation, force majeure (standard) · client
responsibilities · and ONE clear, confident **call to action** (what happens when they say
yes, and by when). Make yes the easy, obvious move.

---

## The winning rubric — score before it reaches the client (nothing below 8)
- **Client understanding** — do they feel truly understood in Section 2?
- **Idea strength** — one distinctive, defensible idea, made vivid?
- **Commercial logic** — money + model make sense; floor held?
- **Market grounding** — real MM/SG numbers, sector-specific, market FX, sourced/tagged?
- **Completeness & polish** — every section full, professional, no placeholders, no typos?
- **Value clarity** — the result and benefit stated as one unmistakable number?
- **Winning close** — is saying yes the obvious next move?
Run it through the roundtable (agents discuss + sharpen) first; fix everything below 8.
