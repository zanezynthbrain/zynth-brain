# The ZYNTH Winning Playbook — why proposals win, and how to make them win

Read this before writing. A complete proposal can still lose; a *winning* one does the
things below. Grounded across all 18 sectors (pull the specifics from the sector packs).

---

## The psychology of "yes" (the order that wins)
A client buys in this sequence — write to it:
1. **"They understand us."** (Section 2 — their world, their words, first.)
2. **"That's a sharp idea."** (Sections 3–4 — the insight + one vivid idea.)
3. **"It will actually work."** (Sections 5–7 — the concrete plan + who executes.)
4. **"It's worth it."** (Sections 8–10 — honest money + an unmistakable result.)
5. **"I trust them."** (Section 12 — proof, dual-market, speed, craft.)
6. **"Yes — let's go."** (Section 14 — one confident, easy next step.)
Skip step 1 and everything after it feels like selling. Nail step 4 or the rest is wasted.

## The seven marks of a winning proposal
1. **Client-first opening** — their challenge before ZYNTH's brilliance.
2. **One ownable idea** — not a tactics menu; something a competitor couldn't have pitched.
3. **Show, don't tell** — the reader sees and feels the work (visual world, the peak moment).
4. **Proof it works** — logic, benchmarks, and a quantified result, not adjectives.
5. **Honest money** — transparent budget, margin held, sell-vs-sponsor decided, not dodged.
6. **The value number** — one headline figure the client can't miss.
7. **An easy yes** — a single, confident call to action with a deadline.

## Anti-patterns that LOSE (never do these)
- A generic deck that could be for any client (no Section 2, no sector specifics).
- A menu of services instead of one idea.
- Vague money ("competitive rates"), or a floor-breaking price hidden by rounding.
- Fabricated stats, contacts, case studies, or logos — instant credibility death.
- Burying the result; making the client do the ROI math themselves.
- A weak close ("let us know your thoughts") instead of a clear next step.
- Adjectives doing a fact's job ("world-class, innovative, cutting-edge").
- **Printing a thin "management fee" line** that exposes/undercuts ZYNTH's margin. Show
  the itemised budget as **client investment by value/workstream** (each line's price
  already includes ZYNTH's margin); hold the 35% floor **silently** (never printed); add
  "investment inclusive of ZYNTH strategy, creative, management & delivery." Do NOT list a
  small "ZYNTH management" line that makes it look like that is all ZYNTH earns.

## Win-themes by sector (what tips the yes)
Pull audience/decision-makers/budgets from the sector packs; lead with the theme:
- **Banking/Fintech** — trust + measurable acquisition (CPA/ROAS with real numbers).
- **Telecom** — youth culture + reach at scale + aownable experiential moment.
- **Real estate** — aspiration + cinematic reveal + qualified leads/bookings on the night.
- **F&B/Hospitality** — desire + social proof + footfall/booking uplift.
- **FMCG** — culture-led activation + sampling-to-social loop + measurable sales lift.
- **Automotive** — the experience (test-drive) + cinematic craft + leads/orders.
- **Tech/SaaS** — MM: installs/actives; SG: pipeline + ABM precision + ROAS.
- **Healthcare/Pharma** — calm authority + real stories + enquiries; compliance respected.
- **Education/EdTech** — outcome + cost-per-enrolment discipline.
- **Retail/E-comm** — tentpole seasonal moment + ROAS.
- **Gov/NGO** — measurable impact + credibility + partnership.
- **Entertainment/Media** — fandom + participation; usually the sponsorship model.
- **Construction/Industrial** — capability proof + qualified B2B leads/tenders.
- **Agriculture** — livelihood stories + trade uptake.
- **Travel/Tourism** — cinematic destination + direct bookings beat OTAs.
- **Insurance** — protection stories + education + enquiries/agent enablement.
- **Logistics** — reliability proof + seller sign-ups.
- **Beauty/Wellness** — transformation + UGC + bookings/sales.

## Sell vs sponsor (decide it, don't dodge it)
- **Sell** when one client owns the outcome and has budget → price the project/retainer.
- **Sponsor-fund** when it's a property multiple brands want to be near → tiered packages
  (Title/Gold/Supporting/In-kind), and **always show each sponsor's own ROI**.
State the decision and the rationale in Section 9 — a proposal that dodges the model reads
as unfinished.

## The finishing pass (before it goes out)
- Run the **roundtable** (agents discuss + sharpen), then score the **winning rubric** —
  nothing below 8, and never below 8 on money or value clarity.
- Read Section 2 as the client: do they feel understood? Read Section 10: is the benefit
  one unmistakable number? Read Section 14: is yes the easy move?
- Proofread. A typo in a proposal signals how you'll run their money. Zero tolerance.
- Every number sourced or `[INDICATIVE]`; every contact real or "to source".
