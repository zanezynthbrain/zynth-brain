## Why this one, and not another spec film

THE BREAK LINE is a good film that ZYNTH can never release. It is spec work for
a brand that has not commissioned us; without Nestlé's approval and a licensed
asset pack it stays in the folder. That is a reasonable thing to have made once.
It is not a reasonable thing to keep making.

This film is the opposite: **ZYNTH's own commercial, for ZYNTH's own brand, with
nothing in it that belongs to anybody else.** It can go on zynth.asia, into a
pitch deck, and onto a feed the day it is cut. It also happens to be the piece
that proves the capability we are selling — an agency that says it produces AI
commercials should have one of its own.

## 1. The idea

**OUT OF CHAOS.**

Every marketing agency says the same two words in a different order: creative,
strategic, data-driven, results. None of it means anything on screen, and a film
that tries to *say* it ends up as stock footage of people pointing at laptops.

So this film does not describe what ZYNTH does. **It performs it, literally, as
a physical event.**

Thousands of gold fragments churn in the dark with no centre and no structure.
Then they stop fighting each other. They converge, they align, and they resolve
into one solid, perfect, finished object.

That is the whole business in fifteen seconds. Clients arrive with noise — a
market they can't read, a brand that says four things at once, a budget with no
shape. What they leave with is one thing that holds together.

### Why this and not the obvious one

The obvious agency film is: fast cuts, a city, a team laughing at a whiteboard,
a graph going up, a logo. It says nothing a competitor could not say next month
with a different logo, because it is about *us* rather than about the work.

This one is ownable because it is a demonstration rather than a claim. And it is
honest about the specific thing ZYNTH sells: not creativity in the abstract, but
**order** — the discipline that turns a mess into something a client can act on.

It has a second layer that we will never state out loud in the film, and don't
need to: the film itself was assembled by a machine, out of noise, into
something composed. The medium is the argument.

### The line

**MM:** "ရှုပ်ထွေးမှုထဲမှ ဖန်တီးမှု။"
**EN:** "Creativity, out of chaos."

Written in Burmese first and transcreated into English, per house rule. Burmese
is **typeset in Pyidaungsu at the endframe — never generated inside the model.**

Endframe lockup: the line, then **ZYNTH — The Intelligence of Creativity.**

## 2. Art direction

| | |
|---|---|
| **Palette** | True black · warm liquid gold · one deep crimson accent. Nothing else enters frame, ever. |
| **Light** | One hard warm key raking low from camera-left so gold reads as molten metal with real specular hits. One crimson rim from behind for separation. Deep falloff to absolute black. |
| **Space** | Infinite void. No floor line, no horizon, no reflection plane. The object exists nowhere, which is why it reads as an idea rather than a product. |
| **Lens** | 100mm long-lens compression, anamorphic feel, shallow depth of field throughout. Fine volumetric haze so one beam is visible. |
| **Motion** | The camera is the only unstable thing in shot 1 and the only still thing in shot 3. Stability is the story. |
| **Sound** | Noise → one pitch → one strike. Music never enters. |

## 3. Shot list

**All three frames derive from a single anchor** (the finished gold form),
generated first and then used as the image2image reference for the other two.
Same gold, same key, same blacks across the film — one shoot, not three renders.

| # | Shot | Movement | Model | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Chaos** — a storm of gold fragments, no centre | Handheld, unstable, searching | Kling 3 Omni | Dry granular metallic hiss and ticking |
| 2 | **Resolve** — particles converge, lattice snaps in | Camera settles into a deliberate arc | Kling 3 Omni | The noise tightens into one pure low tone |
| 3 | **The form** — one solid object, at rest | Almost imperceptible push; only a highlight moves | Gemini Omni Flash | Silence, then ONE resonant strike, then the room |

Shot 3 is composed with **empty black above and below** so the line and the
lockup typeset into it without recomposing.

### The edit rule

The film has exactly one transition idea: **the cut gets calmer every time.**
Shot 1 cuts hard on motion. Shot 2 cuts on the moment the tone arrives. Shot 3
does not cut at all — it is simply allowed to finish.

## 4. Fifteen seconds

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Chaos. Fragments streak past the lens, the camera cannot find a centre. | Granular metallic noise. Restless, directionless. |
| 0:05–0:08 | The swarm turns inward. Lattice lines appear. | The noise tightens; one low tone emerges beneath it. |
| 0:08–0:10 | The core hardens. Edges catch the crimson rim. | The tone gains certainty. Everything else falls away. |
| 0:10–0:11 | **Cut to stillness.** | **Complete silence.** |
| 0:11–0:13 | The finished form, motionless. One highlight drifts. | ONE resonant strike, long natural decay. |
| 0:13–0:15 | Endframe: the line, then the lockup. | The room. Out. |

**The silence at 0:10 is the most important second in the film.** It is the
whole reason the strike lands. Nothing goes in it.

## 5. The sound, and why it is different this time

The three shots were generated on **audio-native models** — the sound in these
files was produced, not written down and hoped for. Two shots on Kling 3 Omni
with `generateSound`, and the payoff shot on Gemini Omni Flash, which gave the
cleanest single-impact SFX in our KitKat comparison.

Every prompt wrote its sound **by exclusion**, explicitly banning music, score,
drums, voice, whoosh, riser, bass drop and stock cinematic impact. Left
unconstrained, all of these models reach for generic trailer score, and that is
what makes AI video sound like AI video. This is the single largest quality
improvement available for free.

## 8. How to assemble it — you need one file, and this is how you get it

Right now this film is **three separate 5-second clips**, each with its own
audio. That is what generation produces. Joining them is an edit, and it has to
happen on a machine that can download the files — the ZYNTH build container's
network policy blocks the OpenArt CDN, so the assembly cannot be done from
inside the agent session.

### The five-minute version — CapCut on your phone

1. Open your OpenArt library and **download all three clips** (chaos, resolve,
   the form) plus the anchor still.
2. CapCut → **New project** → import all three → drop them on the timeline in
   this order: **chaos → resolve → the form**.
3. Trim each clip so the cut lands on movement, not on a pause. Roughly 5s / 5s /
   5s is already right — do not stretch anything.
4. **Do not add music.** The single most damaging thing you can do to this film
   is drop a CapCut track under it. The sound is the idea.
5. Insert **one second of silence** at the very end of the resolve clip, just
   before the form appears. Split the clip, mute the tail. This is the silence
   at 0:10 and it is what makes the strike land.
6. Text at the end, over the black space in the final shot:
   **ရှုပ်ထွေးမှုထဲမှ ဖန်တီးမှု။** then **Creativity, out of chaos.** then
   **ZYNTH — The Intelligence of Creativity.** Use **Pyidaungsu** for the
   Burmese — if CapCut does not have it, typeset that card separately and import
   it as an image.
7. Export **1080p, 24fps, high bitrate**.

That gives you a shareable film today.

### The proper version — DaVinci Resolve

Same order, plus the things CapCut cannot do well:

- **Grade first, look second.** Normalise all three clips to one black point and
  one gold hue before touching the look. This is what makes three generations
  read as one shoot.
- **Fairlight:** the generated audio is raw material, not a mix. Duck the chaos
  hiss under the emerging tone in shot 2, hard-cut to true digital silence for
  one second, then let the strike hit at full range. Master to **−14 LUFS,
  −1.5 dBTP, 48 kHz**.
- **Titles:** typeset in Fusion or as imported PNGs. Never generate Burmese type
  inside a model.
- Export **1080p Rec.709**, then reframe 9:16 and 1:1 from the same timeline.

## 9. What it cost, and what is still missing

**Total spent: 705 OpenArt credits.** Balance 735 → **30**.

| Step | Work | Credits |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | Smart Shot `preview-shot-plan` | 60 |
| Frames | 1 anchor + 2 image2image (Seedream 4.5, 2K) | 45 |
| Shot 1 | Kling 3 Omni, `generateSound` | 175 |
| Shot 2 | Kling 3 Omni, `generateSound` | 175 |
| Shot 3 | Gemini Omni Flash, native audio | 250 |

Produced exactly to `docs/RUNBOOK_COMMERCIAL_FILM.md` — plan first, one anchor,
everything derived, audio-native for every shot the viewer must hear.

**Still missing — honestly:**

1. **Not edited.** Three 5-second clips, not a cut film. The cuts, the grade, the
   mix and the silence at 0:10 are an edit pass in Resolve. That is craft work,
   not generation, and it is where this becomes a film.
2. **No typography yet.** The Burmese line, the English line and the ZYNTH lockup
   are a hand pass at the endframe. Never generated in-frame.
3. **The generated audio is raw material, not a mix.** It gives us real sound to
   cut against instead of silence; levels, ducking and the silence still get set
   by hand.
4. **720p.** Fine for social and review. A 1080p master needs a regeneration pass
   at roughly the same cost.
5. **Not reviewed by ear.** These files carry real audio tracks from audio-native
   models, but nobody at ZYNTH has listened to them yet. Shot 3 is the one to
   check first — if the strike doesn't land, the film doesn't.

**What it would take to finish:** the edit and the typesetting. No further
generation required.
