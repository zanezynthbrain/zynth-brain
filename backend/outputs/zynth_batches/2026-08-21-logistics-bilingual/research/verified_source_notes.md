## Creative and Commercial Boundaries

The batch may use only **generic, client-neutral logistics scenarios** until a client names the commodity class, route, mode, service level, customer segment, data owner and operational constraints. No proposal may state or imply a confirmed carrier, port, terminal, customs arrangement, warehouse, fleet, route, cross-border movement, transit time, pricing, capacity, safety standard, emissions benefit, technology availability, government clearance, partner, customer testimonial or operational performance result.

Any proposed roadshow, warehouse experience, driver programme, trade forum, product demonstration or commercial film must receive client, venue/landowner, safety, operations, privacy, talent, rights and legal approval. High-risk sites, regulated zones, real cargo, live vehicles, driver activity, dangerous goods, customer manifests or customer/employee personal data may not be captured, tested or activated without specialist review and written permission.

> **Research conclusion:** Build the concepts around confidence, visibility, handover, resilience, control and human coordination—not unverified performance promises. The commercial CTA should seek a client-approved consultation, route-planning session, audit, discovery workshop or consented update; it must not promise delivery, price, capacity, compliance or outcome.
