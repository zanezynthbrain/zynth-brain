# ZYNTH Commercial Film
## Everyday Care, Clearly
### Zane-led Myanmar OTC Respiratory Self-Care Campaign

**Document status:** Generation-ready internal treatment for founder and client review. **Product status:** BreatheSure is a fictional placeholder. ## 1. Commercial Brief

The commercial is a premium 60-second brand film for a Myanmar OTC respiratory self-care brand. Zane is the central founder-character and guide, not a doctor or pharmacist. He represents calm strategic clarity: he notices the confusion around an everyday health decision and transforms it into a clear, responsible journey toward approved information and qualified guidance.

The campaign platform is **Everyday Care, Clearly**. The creative device is the **Clear Care Line**, a single warm amber line that moves through the film. It begins as a thin reflection in a dark studio, becomes a path through a home, connects a phone to a pharmacy, and finally resolves into the brand lock-up. The line is a visual metaphor for clarity, not a medical or biological effect.

The intended audience is adults and caregivers making an everyday OTC decision in Myanmar. The story should feel local and recognisable without using a fabricated patient testimonial. The mood moves from uncertainty to calm confidence, never from panic to miracle.

## 3. Creative Treatment

The film opens in a dark, architectural studio. Zane stands before a floating card carrying a single question: **“What should I check first?”** The question is not presented as a medical diagnosis; it is a human decision moment. He touches the card. The Clear Care Line appears and leaves the studio, moving through small, recognisable moments of everyday care.

A parent or adult caregiver sees the question on a phone. The interface simplifies into three prompts: **Check. Ask. Act responsibly.** The film then moves into a warmly lit pharmacy, where a pharmacist character is shown listening and pointing toward approved information. The pharmacist is not shown diagnosing or recommending a specific medicine on camera. Zane remains the guide and visual continuity anchor.

The product pack appears only after the information pathway is established. The pack is a replaceable 3D placeholder with a neutral label area. It must not display invented ingredients, dosage, efficacy, therapeutic claims or a false registration mark. The end card resolves with the campaign line and a legal/medical approval placeholder.

## 4. Character and World Continuity

**Zane:** Original, consistent founder-character in his early-to-mid 30s or the founder’s approved age range. He wears a charcoal tailored overshirt, warm white shirt, dark trousers and one subtle amber detail. He is observant, composed and human. He never wears a white coat, stethoscope or medical credential. His role is strategic guide and responsible brand representative.

**Caregiver/consumer:** One adult Myanmar character, shown in natural home light, using a phone to access approved information. No symptoms are visually exaggerated. Avoid coughing theatrically, breathlessness, panic or child illness imagery.

**Pharmacist:** One qualified-looking adult character in a believable pharmacy environment. The character listens, gestures to approved information and indicates when professional advice is appropriate. The film does not claim the actor is a medical professional.

**World palette:** Graphite and midnight blue at the opening; warm ivory and natural wood in the home; teal and amber in the information system; clean white and soft daylight in the pharmacy. The Clear Care Line is always amber and never changes into a red “danger” signal.

## 8. Art Direction and VFX Rules

The film should use practical-feeling environments enhanced by restrained visual effects. The Clear Care Line may pass through reflections, screens and architectural surfaces, but it must never enter a person’s body, illuminate lungs, erase symptoms, repair tissue or imply a pharmacological mechanism. Those images would create an unsupported medical meaning.

The pharmacy should feel credible without using a real chain’s branding. Any signage, uniforms or shelf labels must be fictional or cleared. The product pack must remain a replaceable 3D asset. The final production version must use the registered product’s approved artwork and must not fabricate a registration number or certification symbol.

## 9. Audio and Voice Direction

The score begins with sparse piano and glass tones, develops into warm strings and a subtle electronic pulse, then resolves with human room tone and one open final chord. The audio journey should communicate thoughtful progress rather than emergency or miraculous cure.

The voiceover should be delivered by a calm, credible adult voice. It should sound like a trusted guide, not a doctor, announcer or hard-sell salesperson. Avoid urgency, fear, exaggerated breath sounds and absolute words such as “cures,” “guarantees,” “instant,” “safe for everyone” or “no side effects.”
