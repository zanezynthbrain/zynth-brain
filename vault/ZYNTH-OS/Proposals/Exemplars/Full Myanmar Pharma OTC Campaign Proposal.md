## 1. Executive summary

Myanmar’s OTC market creates a strong communication opportunity for a pharmaceutical company that can make self-care information feel clearer, more trustworthy and more useful at the moment of consideration. A Yangon-focused study on OTC purchasing behaviour identifies reputation, price, availability, safety, effectiveness, prior experience and advice from family members or pharmacy staff as relevant influences on purchase and repeat behaviour. It also identifies digital marketing and professional recommendation as variables requiring attention, while cautioning that health literacy and access to reliable expert counsel vary. [3]

ZYNTH proposes **Everyday Care, Clearly**, a 12-week integrated consumer-health campaign for a registered OTC respiratory self-care product. The campaign does not make a dramatic efficacy promise. Instead, it builds a distinctive trust platform around **clear information, responsible self-care and confident next steps**. The system connects short-form educational content, pharmacy-counter communication, pharmacist-support materials, a mobile “Clear Care Counter” activation, a question-led social content world and a measurement loop.

The campaign’s commercial role is to improve qualified awareness and consideration among adult self-care shoppers and caregivers, strengthen the quality of the brand experience at pharmacy, and create a repeatable platform the client can extend across seasonal demand moments. It is not designed to replace a physician, pharmacist, diagnosis or product label. It directs consumers to use approved information and seek qualified care where appropriate.

| Decision required from client | Recommended answer |
|---|---|
| Which problem should the brand own? | Confusion and uncertainty at the moment of everyday respiratory self-care consideration. |
| What should the campaign make people feel? | “I understand the responsible next step.” |
| What should the brand be remembered for? | Clear, dependable self-care information and an easy-to-recognise pharmacy experience. |
| What should happen commercially? | More qualified consideration, stronger pharmacy recommendation conditions, clearer product understanding and a measurable route to repeat activity. |
| What must not happen? | Unsupported health claims, diagnosis by content, fear-based persuasion, unsafe self-medication, undisclosed paid endorsements or public release before regulatory approval. |

## 2. Campaign title, concept and proposition

### Campaign title

# Everyday Care, Clearly

### Concept

When people are deciding how to respond to a common everyday respiratory concern, uncertainty can be louder than information. **Everyday Care, Clearly** turns the brand into a calm guide: it makes the difference between a vague worry and a responsible next step visible through simple, approved information delivered where people already make decisions.

The campaign uses a recurring visual device called the **Clear Care Line**. In every execution, a thin line moves through a moment of confusion and connects three practical points: **understand the situation, check the approved information, choose the appropriate next step**. The line is not a medical diagnosis and does not imply that the product is right for every person. It is a visual system for clarity and responsible action.

### Single-minded proposition

> **For everyday respiratory self-care decisions, BreatheSure helps make the next step clearer—using only approved product information and responsible guidance.**

This line remains subject to final medical/legal/regulatory review and must be rewritten if it exceeds the product’s approved indications or claims.

### Creative expression

The campaign world is warm, clear and human rather than clinical or alarmist. A household, a pharmacy counter, a commute, a school-run moment and a working day are shown with restrained realism. The brand’s visual system uses white space, deep teal, warm amber and a clear coral accent, with bilingual Myanmar/English layout only after professional translation and client approval.

## 4. Business context and opportunity

OTC purchase decisions are not created by advertising alone. They are shaped by the product’s perceived reliability, affordability, availability, previous experience, family influence, pharmacy advice, professional recommendation, information clarity and the consumer’s confidence that the product is appropriate for the situation. The Yangon study used these variables to examine OTC purchase decisions and post-purchase behaviour; its findings should be treated as directional local evidence, not as a national market census. [3]

This creates four commercial opportunities for a responsible pharmaceutical brand:

| Opportunity | ZYNTH interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Clarity at the shelf** | Make approved product information easier to understand without simplifying away warnings or creating a diagnosis. |
| **Pharmacy confidence** | Give pharmacy teams a consistent, useful and compliant communication toolkit rather than a vague promotional script. |
| **Digital reassurance** | Build a repeatable content platform that answers responsible self-care questions and directs people to qualified support when needed. |
| **Learning-led scale** | Pilot in selected Yangon and Mandalay pharmacy clusters, measure qualified behaviours, then decide whether to scale. |

### Competitive implication

The campaign should not compete only on louder claims, celebrity reach or price promotion. It should build a branded system of **clear information, dependable experience and responsible next-step guidance**. This is more defensible for a regulated product and gives the client reusable brand equity beyond one seasonal burst.

### Strategic problem statement

> Consumers may encounter many health messages, but a regulated brand can earn preference by making approved information, pharmacy interaction and responsible next steps feel clearer and more dependable than category noise.

## 6. Audience, journey and human insight

### Primary audience

The primary audience is adults aged approximately 25–45 in Yangon and Mandalay who make or influence everyday household self-care purchases. This includes working adults, caregivers and adults responsible for purchasing for parents or family members. The campaign must not imply that every symptom should be self-treated or that a product replaces professional medical guidance.

### Secondary audience

The secondary audience is pharmacy staff and qualified healthcare professionals who may influence product consideration. Their role is not to deliver an unapproved sales script. Their role is to receive a compliant product-information and consumer-guidance toolkit supplied and approved by the client’s medical/regulatory team.

### Tertiary audience

The tertiary audience includes household influencers and digital participants who share health information. Their content must be moderated carefully because health misinformation can travel through private groups and informal online sources.

### Journey model

| Journey stage | Consumer question | ZYNTH communication role |
|---|---|---|
| **Recognise** | “What is happening, and should I pay attention?” | Plain-language educational content with no diagnosis. |
| **Check** | “What information should I read before choosing?” | Approved label-linked information, warnings, appropriate-use prompts and pharmacy guidance. |
| **Choose** | “Where can I find a legitimate product and ask a useful question?” | Pharmacy locator, approved point-of-sale information and responsible staff interaction. |
| **Use responsibly** | “How do I follow the approved information, and when should I seek help?” | Client-approved usage information, warning signs and escalation direction. |
| **Remember** | “Would I trust this brand again?” | Reliable experience, clear information, respectful tone and post-purchase feedback. |

### Human insight

> **When people are looking after themselves or someone they love, confidence does not come from the loudest promise. It comes from knowing what to check and what to do next.**

This insight is a strategic hypothesis to validate during discovery; it is not presented as a universal clinical or consumer truth.

## 7. Strategic approach

ZYNTH recommends a three-part strategy called **Clear the Moment**.

### 7.1 Make approved information easier to notice

Transform the client’s approved product information into a hierarchy that people can use quickly: what the product is for, who should not use it, what to check, where to ask, and when to seek qualified help. The content team will not rewrite medical claims independently; it will translate approved information into clear creative formats for review.

### 7.2 Connect digital clarity to pharmacy action

Every major digital asset points toward one responsible next step: read approved information, locate a participating pharmacy, ask a pharmacist, or seek professional care when the situation requires it. The campaign should not create a disconnected social burst with no route to legitimate availability or helpful information.

### 7.3 Build a repeatable trust experience

The same Clear Care Line, colour language, question format and disclaimer structure appear across short-form video, pharmacy materials, activation design and the information destination. This creates memory without turning the campaign into a single product claim.

### Strategic principles

| Principle | Application |
|---|---|
| **Truth before persuasion** | Every claim maps to client-approved source material. |
| **Responsibility before reach** | Content that increases confusion, shame or unsafe self-treatment is rejected even if it appears attention-grabbing. |
| **Local usefulness** | Myanmar-first language, pharmacy realities, urban and regional context and accessible formats. |
| **Human confidence** | Warm, calm and respectful storytelling instead of fear, exaggerated urgency or miracle language. |
| **Test before scale** | Pilot, measure, correct and only then expand. |

## 8. Creative platform and campaign system

### The Clear Care Line

The Clear Care Line is a moving visual line that represents a responsible path through a moment of uncertainty. In a 10-second video, it may move from a cluttered kitchen table to the correct approved information card. In a pharmacy display, it may connect a question card to a product-information panel. In an activation, it may guide a visitor through three stations: **Check**, **Ask**, **Act responsibly**.

### Core content territories

| Territory | Example content form | Compliance control |
|---|---|---|
| **Check the label** | Short visual explainer showing how to locate approved product information. | Client medical/regulatory approval of every label reference. |
| **Ask well** | Pharmacy-question prompts such as what to check before choosing a product. | No diagnosis; pharmacist or client review of script. |
| **Know the next step** | Scenario stories showing a person choosing to seek qualified care when appropriate. | Warning signs and escalation language must be client-approved. |
| **Care without confusion** | Household and caregiver moments where clarity reduces stress. | No implied guaranteed outcome; no vulnerable-person exploitation. |
| **Clear Care Counter** | Pharmacy activation and compliant display toolkit. | Registration, distribution, pharmacy and display permissions confirmed. |

### Campaign line options for approval

The preferred line is **“Everyday care, clearly.”** Alternative internal options are **“Know the next step.”** and **“Clear information. Responsible care.”** Final wording must be checked against the product’s approved claims and legal requirements.

## 9. Channel and activation architecture

### 9.1 Digital content system

The digital programme uses Facebook, TikTok, YouTube Shorts and an approved information destination according to the client’s actual media access and regulatory review. The content mix is designed as a system rather than a post count.

| Content type | Role | Indicative 12-week quantity |
|---|---|---:|
| Hero campaign film | Establish the trust platform and visual memory. | 1 x 30–45 second master; cutdowns subject to approval. |
| Short-form explainers | Make one approved information point clear per asset. | 12–18 vertical videos. |
| Question cards | Invite safe, useful questions rather than diagnosis. | 12 static/carousel assets. |
| Pharmacy guidance content | Show how to ask and where to find approved information. | 6 assets. |
| Responsible-care scenarios | Demonstrate next-step thinking without making clinical promises. | 6 assets. |
| Community moderation protocol | Classify, answer, escalate or remove unsafe health questions/content. | 1 operating protocol plus moderation log. |

### 9.2 Clear Care Counter

The activation is a modular pharmacy experience that can operate in selected participating points. It includes an approved information panel, three question prompts, a clean product-information route, optional QR access to the approved destination and a pharmacy staff briefing card. It must never pressure a pharmacy worker to recommend a medicine outside their professional role or the approved product information.

### 9.3 Pharmacy network and field execution

The client supplies the potential pharmacy/distributor list, legal permissions, stock and point-of-sale requirements. ZYNTH prepares the activation concept, toolkit, training structure, field checklist and reporting model. Any field partner, merchandiser or promoter must receive a written role, conduct standard, claim boundary, escalation route and incident-reporting form.

### 9.4 Optional professional education module

A separate module may create approved educational materials for pharmacists or healthcare professionals. It should not be mixed casually with consumer influencer content. Any professional engagement requires client medical/regulatory direction, appropriate credentials, documented consent and transparent role disclosure.

### 9.5 Owned destination

The campaign should use a lightweight, mobile-first information destination or approved client page containing only reviewed content: product information, responsible-use guidance, warnings, approved FAQ, participating pharmacy locator if accurate, contact route and a clear professional-care direction when appropriate. ZYNTH does not publish medical content without the client approval chain.

## 11. Implementation plan and timeline

### Phase 0 — Discovery and compliance lock: Weeks 1–2

ZYNTH confirms the product, registration, approved label, approved indications, evidence, distribution, target geography, prior campaign data, media access, pharmacy partners and decision owners. The team develops the claim matrix, audience hypotheses, measurement plan and initial creative territories. Nothing is released externally.

### Phase 1 — Creative and operational development: Weeks 3–4

ZYNTH produces the approved campaign platform, content scripts, hero film treatment, pharmacy toolkit, activation design, destination copy deck and field protocol. The client’s medical, regulatory, legal and brand reviewers assess each asset. Suppliers are briefed for quotations only; no booking occurs before founder/client approval.

### Phase 2 — Pilot activation: Weeks 5–8

The campaign launches only in approved channels and participating pharmacy points. Digital content, moderation, pharmacy execution and approved information destination operate as one system. A weekly review checks compliance, content quality, question themes, availability and leading indicators.

### Phase 3 — Optimisation and scale decision: Weeks 9–12

ZYNTH compares content and activation performance to baseline, reviews pharmacy and consumer feedback, logs incidents and proposes a scale, revise or stop decision. A scale plan may include additional cities, seasonal relevance, additional content territories or a longer Growth Engine retainer.

## 12. Measurement and learning system

### Measurement framework

| Layer | Metric examples | Source and owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivery** | Assets approved, assets released, pharmacy points activated, staff briefings completed, stock/availability status. | ZYNTH delivery log and client field report. |
| **Attention quality** | Three-second views, completion rate, saves, meaningful comments, shares, approved FAQ interactions. | Platform analytics and moderation log. |
| **Consideration** | Product-information page visits, approved QR scans, pharmacy locator use, information downloads, qualified questions. | Client-approved analytics setup. |
| **Experience** | Pharmacy staff feedback, activation completion, question themes, consumer clarity rating. | Structured field feedback and intercept method if approved. |
| **Trust and safety** | Complaint themes, misinformation flags, claim incidents, escalation time, removed content and unresolved cases. | Compliance and community log. |
| **Commercial proxy** | Availability, retailer/stock signals, approved brand-search movement, product consideration or sales data where client can provide it. | Client data; no unsupported attribution. |

### Testing plan

The first two weeks establish baseline and identify which approved information points create useful engagement. Weeks 5–6 test content framing, not medical efficacy. Possible tests include question-led versus scenario-led creative, short versus medium video, Myanmar-only versus bilingual layout, and different calls to read approved information or locate a pharmacy. Every test must preserve the same approved claim and responsible-care boundary.

### Reporting cadence

ZYNTH provides a weekly operating note, a biweekly optimisation review and a final 12-week decision report. Reporting distinguishes **observed result**, **client-provided data**, **directional signal**, **hypothesis** and **unknown**.

## 15. Risks, assumptions, next steps and decision request
