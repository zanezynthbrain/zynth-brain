# Built to be Trusted — Myanmar Manufacturing Sector Campaign ★ full client-ready

**Sector.** Manufacturing & Industrial

**Market.** Myanmar (Yangon, Mandalay, Thilawa SEZ)

**Type.** Integrated B2B campaign — brand authority + demand generation

## 1. CAMPAIGN OVERVIEW

Myanmar manufacturers sell on price because nobody has given buyers another
reason to choose. Procurement managers at distributors, hotel groups, and
construction firms compare quotes on a spreadsheet — and a spreadsheet only has
a column for cost. The manufacturer with the better factory loses to the one
with the lower number, and then both are squeezed again next quarter.

This campaign moves one manufacturer out of that column.

**Objective.** 120 qualified inbound enquiries and 25 factory visits over 16
weeks, at a cost per qualified lead at or below **MMK 45,000** — against a
Yangon B2B outbound benchmark of roughly MMK 110,000 per meeting.

**Period.** 16 weeks. **Budget envelope.** MMK 62,000,000.

**The bet.** In a market where every competitor claims quality, the one who
*shows* it wins. Not a slogan — an open factory.

## 2. TARGET AUDIENCE PROFILE

### Segment A — The Procurement Manager
**Demographics.** 30–45, mixed gender, mid-senior at a distributor, hotel group,
construction firm or FMCG brand. Signs or shortlists for MMK 20–500M annually.
**Location.** Yangon (Downtown, Bahan), Mandalay industrial belt.
**Psychographics.** Personally accountable for a failure nobody remembers when
it goes right. Will pay 8–12% more for a supplier who will not embarrass them.
**Behaviours.** Researches on Facebook and Viber, decides in person. Asks peers
before asking Google. Wants a factory visit before a first order.
**Pain points.** Late delivery with no warning; inconsistent batch quality; no
documentation for their own auditors; suppliers who vanish when a defect appears.
**Media.** Facebook 8–10 PM, Viber business groups all day, LinkedIn rarely,
industry WhatsApp groups constantly. Trade press: Myanmar Business Today.

### Segment B — The Factory Owner / Managing Director
**Demographics.** 40–60, owner-operator, 50–400 staff, second generation common.
**Psychographics.** Proud of the plant, frustrated it is invisible. Believes
marketing is for consumer brands and has been sold three websites that did nothing.
**Pain points.** Losing tenders to cheaper, worse competitors; no way to prove
capability without a site visit; every enquiry arrives through a personal contact.
**Media.** Facebook, Viber, chamber-of-commerce events, WhatsApp.

### Segment C — The International Sourcing Agent
**Demographics.** 28–45, based Singapore/Bangkok, sourcing for regional buyers.
**Psychographics.** Risk-averse on Myanmar. Needs evidence of compliance,
capacity and continuity before recommending a supplier upward.
**Pain points.** Cannot verify claims remotely; no English documentation;
unclear certifications; no idea if the factory can hold volume.
**Media.** LinkedIn, email, industry directories, video walkthroughs.

## 3. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Myanmar industrial marketing is close to a vacuum, which is the opportunity.

| Who | What they do now | The gap we take |
|---|---|---|
| Local manufacturers | A Facebook page with product photos, updated rarely | No proof, no process, no people |
| Regional importers (Thai, Chinese) | Polished catalogues, real certifications | Long lead times, no local service, no relationship |
| Trading intermediaries | Own the buyer relationship, hide the factory | We put the factory in front of the buyer directly |

Nobody in this category is publishing evidence. The first manufacturer to show
its floor, its process and its people owns "trustworthy" before anyone else
thinks to compete for it.

## 4. CAMPAIGN STRATEGY

### The big idea — "Built to be Trusted" / "ယုံကြည်ရဖို့ တည်ဆောက်ထားတယ်"

Every competitor says quality. We do not say it. **We open the doors.**

The campaign is one long act of transparency: the factory floor filmed without
staging, the QC bench shown mid-rejection, the line supervisor explaining the
tolerance in her own words, the delivery log published monthly whether it is
good or bad. Trust is not claimed. It is demonstrated, publicly, repeatedly.

**Why this and not the obvious one.** The obvious campaign is a corporate film
with drone shots, a handshake, and "Quality You Can Trust". It is beautiful, it
is forgettable, and a competitor can copy it in a month. An open factory cannot
be copied by anyone who is not actually confident in their floor — which is the
whole point, and the reason it defends the price premium.

**Brand positioning.** The supplier you can send your auditor to unannounced.

**Key messages.**
- "Come and see." — an open-door standing invitation, not a campaign line.
- "Every batch documented." — QC records issued with delivery, to the buyer's auditors.
- "The people who make it." — named operators, not stock photography.
- "On time, on record." — delivery performance published monthly, including misses.

**Tone of voice.** Plain, specific, unhurried. Numbers over adjectives. Burmese
written first (professional register, not colloquial); English transcreated for
Segment C. Never boastful — the evidence carries it.

**Hashtags.** Primary `#BuiltToBeTrusted` · Secondary `#မြန်မာ့ထုတ်လုပ်မှု` `#OpenFactoryMM`

## 9. ACTIVATION — OPEN FACTORY DAYS

Four Open Factory Days across the campaign, 25–40 buyers each.

Buyers arrive without a curated route. They walk the floor with the supervisor,
watch a live QC rejection, see the documentation system, and leave with a sample
and a batch record carrying their own name on it. No presentation room, no
slides. The factory is the pitch.

Plus presence at **two trade fairs** (Myanmar Build & Décor, Manufacturing
Myanmar) — a working demonstration bench, not a brochure counter.
