# Worth the Trip — Myanmar Retail & Shopping Mall Footfall Campaign ★ full client-ready

**Sector.** Retail & Shopping Malls (mall operator or multi-store retail group)

**Market.** Myanmar (Yangon, Mandalay)

**Type.** Integrated footfall campaign — weekday traffic, dwell time, tenant yield

## 1. CAMPAIGN OVERVIEW

Myanmar malls have a weekday problem that discounts cannot fix.

Saturday is full. Sunday is full. Tuesday afternoon the corridors are empty, the
tenants are paying rent against a floor that is not converting, and the operator's
answer is another sale — which pulls the same weekend crowd forward, cannibalises
Saturday, and trains everyone to wait for the next promotion.

Online shopping has already taken the *errand*. What it cannot take is the trip
that is worth making for its own sake.

**Objective.** Lift Tuesday–Thursday footfall by **28%**, increase average dwell
time by 16 minutes, and raise tenant-reported weekday conversion by 9 points —
over 16 weeks, without a mall-wide discount event.

**Period.** 16 weeks. **Budget envelope.** MMK 72,000,000.

**The bet.** People do not come to a mall to buy. They come to *be somewhere*.
Give them a reason to be somewhere on a Wednesday and the buying follows.

## 2. TARGET AUDIENCE PROFILE

### Segment A — The Weekday-Available Parent
**Demographics.** 28–45, majority women, one or two school-age children, not in
fixed office hours — small business, freelance, or family-managed work.
**Location.** Within 6km of the centre — Yangon (Hlaing, Insein, Thingangyun,
South Okkalapa), Mandalay Chan Aye Thar Zan and Maha Aung Myay.
**Psychographics.** The 10 AM–2 PM window is genuinely free and genuinely empty.
Wants somewhere air-conditioned, safe and cheap to spend it, and feels slightly
guilty about "just going to the mall".
**Behaviours.** Decides that morning, not in advance. Goes where a friend is
going. Buys incidentally, not deliberately.
**Pain points.** Nothing to do that is not spending; children bored in twenty
minutes; food court the only reason to stay; no reason to choose Tuesday.
**Media.** Facebook 8–11 AM and 8–10 PM, Viber mother-and-neighbour groups, TikTok.

### Segment B — The Office Worker Within Walking Distance
**Demographics.** 23–38, offices in and around the centre, 60–90 minute lunch.
**Psychographics.** Lunch is the only free hour of the day. Currently spends it
at the same three places.
**Pain points.** Queues at 12:15; nothing to do after eating; no reason to return
in the evening.
**Media.** Facebook, TikTok at lunch, workplace Viber groups.

### Segment C — The Older Weekday Visitor
**Demographics.** 55+, retired or semi-retired, often with grandchildren.
**Psychographics.** Wants routine, cool air, seating and company. A reliable
weekly reason to leave the house is worth more than any promotion.
**Pain points.** Nowhere to sit without buying; nothing scheduled; feels the
space is not for them.
**Media.** Facebook, Viber family groups, word of mouth, community networks.

### Segment D — The Tenant (internal, and the actual client of the result)
**Demographics.** Store managers and owners across 60–140 units.
**Psychographics.** Sceptical of mall marketing because past campaigns drove
footfall that never entered their store.
**Pain points.** Pays marketing levy, sees no attributable return; not told what
is happening until it happens; cannot staff for traffic nobody forecast.
**Why they are an audience.** A campaign the tenants do not know about converts
at a fraction of one they helped plan.

## 3. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

| Who | What they do | The gap we take |
|---|---|---|
| Other malls | Weekend events, seasonal sales, celebrity appearances | All competing for the same Saturday; weekday is conceded |
| Delivery + e-commerce | Convenience, price, no trip required | Cannot give you somewhere to be |
| Street markets | Price, proximity, social familiarity | Heat, rain, no seating, no safety for children |
| Cafés and co-working | The weekday hang-out, small scale | Cannot host a family or a hundred people |

Every operator is fighting for the weekend and abandoning four days a week. The
weekday is not a weak market — it is an unclaimed one.

## 4. CAMPAIGN STRATEGY

### The big idea — "Worth the Trip" / "လာရကျိုးနပ်တယ်"

Stop advertising what is on sale. **Programme the week.**

Every weekday gets a fixed, free, recurring reason to come — the same thing, the
same time, every week, for the whole campaign and beyond. Not events. A
**timetable**. A mall people can plan their week around, the way they plan around
a class or a market day.

| Day | 10:30 AM | 5:30 PM |
|---|---|---|
| **Tuesday** | Children's story hour, Burmese, free | Homework corner — supervised, free tables |
| **Wednesday** | Skills bench — a tenant teaches something in 40 min | Repair table — bring one broken thing |
| **Thursday** | Elders' morning — tea, seating, live radio-style music | Language exchange, Burmese–English |

Free, recurring, unbranded by any single tenant, and cheap. The commercial return
is not in the programme; it is in the 16 extra minutes of dwell and the incidental
purchase on the way out.

**Why this and not the obvious one.** The obvious campaign is "MEGA WEEKDAY SALE
— UP TO 50% OFF". It moves margin, not people, it trains the customer to wait for
the next one, and any competitor can outbid it next month. A timetable cannot be
outbid — it can only be out-committed, and a competitor who copies it arrives
second into a habit that is already formed.

**Brand positioning.** The place your week has a reason to include.

**Key messages.**
- "Every Tuesday at 10:30." — specificity is the entire mechanic.
- "Free. No purchase. Just come." — the barrier removed out loud.
- "Somewhere to be, not something to buy." — permission for Segment A's guilt.
- "Your tenants are teaching it." — the skills bench, tenant-led.

**Tone of voice.** Neighbourly, concrete, unhurried. Burmese first, everyday
register. Times and days in every asset — no aspirational lifestyle copy. The
creative unit is a schedule, not a slogan.

**Hashtags.** Primary `#လာရကျိုးနပ်တယ်` · Secondary `#WorthTheTrip` `#EveryTuesday1030`

## 9. ACTIVATION — THE TIMETABLE ITSELF

The programme *is* the activation. Six recurring slots, three days, sixteen weeks
— **96 sessions**, not six events.

Physical build: a permanent painted timetable board at each entrance, a
free-seating zone with 40 chairs that requires no purchase, a homework corner
with tables and plugs, and a repair table with basic tools.

The free-seating zone is the most contested item in this proposal and the one we
will defend hardest. Every mall operator's instinct is that seating without
purchase is lost revenue. It is the precondition for Segment C existing at all,
and Segment C brings grandchildren and buys food.

## 13. ROI PROJECTION

Baseline weekday footfall: 4,200/day. Conversion 31%, average transaction MMK
18,000, operator share via rent + levy + turnover clauses ≈ 9%.

| Scenario | Footfall lift | Incremental weekday visits/yr | Incremental tenant sales (MMK) | Operator share | Return on 153M |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | +18% | 196,000 | 1,094,000,000 | 98,000,000 | 0.6× yr 1 |
| Base | +28% | 305,000 | 1,702,000,000 | 153,000,000 | 1.0× yr 1 |
| Upside | +36% | 392,000 | 2,188,000,000 | 197,000,000 | 1.3× yr 1 |

**Read this honestly: the base case pays back in roughly twelve months, not in
twelve weeks.** This is a habit-formation campaign and the arithmetic reflects
that. The real return is in year two, when the timetable continues at
approximately MMK 15,200,000 per quarter in delivery cost with no build and no
launch media — at which point the same footfall costs about a fifth as much.

If the operator needs payback inside the campaign window, this is the wrong
campaign and a discount event is the right one. We would rather say that now.

## 14. RISK & MITIGATION

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A session is cancelled or moved | Medium | **High** | Reliability is the entire product. Contracted delivery staff with named substitutes; a cancelled slot runs anyway with mall staff. Never move a time. | ZYNTH + mall ops |
| Free seating is read as lost commercial space | **High** | Medium | Measure spend-per-visitor in the seating zone from week 1; the data settles the argument, not opinion | Mall commercial |
| Weekend footfall falls (cannibalisation) | Medium | High | Weekly monitoring against the ≤0% guardrail; weekday-only creative and targeting | ZYNTH |
| Tenants do not staff for weekday traffic | **High** | High | Forecasts shared weekly in advance; the tenant programme exists for this | Mall leasing |
| Child safety incident in a free programme | Low | **Severe** | Guardian remains present and responsible — stated in every asset; supervised zones; trained staff; incident protocol agreed before week 4 | Mall ops |
| Programme attracts loitering complaints from tenants | Medium | Medium | Zones sited by leasing, not by marketing; monthly tenant review | Mall leasing |
| Rainy season suppresses trips | **High** | Medium | Rain is an *advantage* for an indoor timetable — creative pivots to it rather than around it | ZYNTH |
