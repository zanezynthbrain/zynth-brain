# The Hours You Don't Lose — Myanmar Solar & Backup Power Campaign ★ full client-ready

**Sector.** Energy & Renewables (solar + backup power)

**Market.** Myanmar (Yangon, Mandalay, Hlaing Tharyar & Shwe Pyi Thar industrial zones)

**Type.** Integrated B2B + B2C campaign — demand generation and category education

## 1. CAMPAIGN OVERVIEW

Every solar company in Myanmar sells the same thing: a payback period. "Recover
your investment in 3.5 years." Buyers have heard it from six vendors, the numbers
never match, and the assumption underneath — that the grid stays as it is — is
the one thing nobody can promise.

So the buyer stalls. Not because solar is wrong, but because the argument is
unverifiable and everyone makes it.

**This campaign changes the unit of measurement.**

**Objective.** 180 qualified site surveys and 45 signed installations over 16
weeks, at a cost per qualified survey at or below **MMK 95,000**.

**Period.** 16 weeks. **Budget envelope.** MMK 68,000,000.

**The bet.** Nobody can verify a payback claim in advance. Everybody can count
the hours their business stopped last month. Sell the hours.

## 2. TARGET AUDIENCE PROFILE

### Segment A — The Factory / Workshop Owner (highest value)
**Demographics.** 35–58, owner or GM of a garment factory, cold store, printing
works, food processor or plastics plant. 40–600 staff. Diesel bill MMK 3–20M/month.
**Location.** Hlaing Tharyar, Shwe Pyi Thar, Mingaladon, Mandalay industrial belt.
**Psychographics.** Has been burned by an underspecified system that failed in
year two. Trusts the engineer who shows up, not the salesman who calls.
**Behaviours.** Asks other owners first. Wants to visit a working installation of
similar size before committing. Decides slowly, then buys fully.
**Pain points.** Production stops mid-shift; diesel cost and theft; generator
maintenance downtime; no way to compare vendor quotes because none use the same
assumptions; fear of being sold panels that underperform in monsoon.
**Media.** Viber business groups, Facebook, industry associations, WhatsApp with
their peers. Rarely searches; asks.

### Segment B — The Commercial Building / Hotel Operator
**Demographics.** 30–50, GM or facilities head of a hotel, clinic, mall unit,
office building, private school. 
**Psychographics.** Outages are a guest-experience problem before they are a cost
problem. A dark lobby is a review.
**Pain points.** Generator noise and fumes near guests; fuel logistics; peak
tariff exposure; no visibility on where power actually goes in the building.
**Media.** Facebook, LinkedIn, industry associations, direct sales relationships.

### Segment C — The Household Buyer (volume, brand-building)
**Demographics.** 28–50, homeowner, dual-income, Yangon/Mandalay suburbs.
**Psychographics.** Wants the fridge, the fan, the wifi and the children's study
lamp to survive the cut. Not interested in kilowatts.
**Pain points.** Doesn't know what size to buy; afraid of being oversold; no
trusted installer; unclear warranty and who services it in year three.
**Media.** Facebook 8–10 PM, TikTok, YouTube for explainers, Viber for quotes.

## 3. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

| Who | What they sell | The gap we take |
|---|---|---|
| Established solar EPCs | Payback-period math, technical specs | Nobody can verify the claim before buying |
| Cheap import traders | Lowest price per panel | No design, no service, no accountability at year three |
| Generator suppliers | Familiarity, instant power | Fuel cost, noise, maintenance — the incumbent to displace |
| Regional/Chinese EPCs | Scale and price on large tenders | No local service depth, slow response |

Every player argues on price or specification. Nobody has claimed **continuity**
— the thing the buyer actually wants — and nobody publishes what their installed
systems delivered after the fact.

## 4. CAMPAIGN STRATEGY

### The big idea — "The Hours You Don't Lose" / "မဆုံးရှုံးတဲ့ နာရီများ"

Stop selling kilowatts. Stop selling payback. **Sell the hours.**

Every proposal, every ad, every case study is denominated in one number: the
hours this business kept running that it would otherwise have lost. A factory
does not experience 12 kWp. It experiences a shift that did not stop.

The campaign's engine is the **Hours Counter** — a published, per-installation
running total of outage hours covered, updated monthly from real inverter data
across the client's installed base. It is a number that only a company with real
installations can show, and it grows every month without new advertising.

**Why this and not the obvious one.** The obvious campaign is panels on a roof,
a sunrise, and "Power Your Future". It is interchangeable, it competes on price,
and it forces the payback argument the buyer has stopped believing. The Hours
Counter cannot be run by a trader with no installed base, cannot be faked, and
converts every past customer into ongoing proof.

**Brand positioning.** The power company that counts what you keep, not what you spend.

**Key messages.**
- "Your last month, in hours." — a free outage audit that quantifies the loss.
- "See it running." — visit a working installation your own size before you buy.
- "Same assumptions, every quote." — a published quoting methodology so vendors can finally be compared.
- "Year three is the test." — service record and warranty made the headline, not the footnote.

**Tone of voice.** Engineer-plain. Burmese first, everyday register for Segment C,
technical-professional for A and B. No sunrise language, no "green future"
abstraction — this market buys reliability, not sentiment.

**Hashtags.** Primary `#မဆုံးရှုံးတဲ့နာရီများ` · Secondary `#HoursYouDontLose` `#SeeItRunning`

## 9. ACTIVATION — SEE IT RUNNING

**Six Site Visit Days** across the campaign. Prospective buyers are taken to a
working installation of comparable size — a garment factory owner visits a
garment factory, not a showroom. They meet the owner without a salesperson in
the room, see the inverter data, and hear what went wrong during commissioning.

Plus presence at **two industry fairs** (Myanmar Energy Expo, Manufacturing
Myanmar) with a live load-shedding demonstration rig: the grid is cut on the
stand, every hour, and the stand stays lit.
