# The Promise You Can Track — Myanmar Logistics & Delivery Campaign ★ full client-ready

**Sector.** Logistics & Supply Chain

**Market.** Myanmar (Yangon, Mandalay, the Yangon–Mandalay corridor)

**Type.** Integrated B2B + B2C campaign — trust building and demand generation

## 1. CAMPAIGN OVERVIEW

Every logistics company in Myanmar advertises speed. None of them advertises
honesty. The result is a market where the customer assumes the delivery date is
optimistic, the merchant assumes the courier will lose something eventually, and
both plan around a promise they do not believe.

That disbelief is expensive. Merchants hold extra stock. Customers refuse
prepayment. Both cost the operator margin.

**Objective.** 4,000 new merchant accounts and a 22-point lift in delivery-promise
trust over 14 weeks, at a cost per acquired merchant at or below **MMK 38,000**.

**Period.** 14 weeks. **Budget envelope.** MMK 54,000,000.

**The bet.** Do not promise faster. Promise *accurately*, then publish the score.
The operator who is honest about a two-day delivery beats the one who claims one
day and misses.

## 2. TARGET AUDIENCE PROFILE

### Segment A — The Online Seller (primary revenue)
**Demographics.** 22–38, majority women, running a Facebook or TikTok shop —
fashion, cosmetics, phone accessories, home goods. 30–400 parcels a month.
**Location.** Yangon (Insein, Hlaing, Thingangyun), Mandalay, secondary towns.
**Psychographics.** Their reputation *is* the courier's performance. One lost
parcel becomes a public comment thread under their next post.
**Behaviours.** Compares couriers in seller Facebook groups. Switches after two
bad experiences. Chooses on COD remittance speed as much as delivery speed.
**Pain points.** Cash-on-delivery money released slowly; no proof of a failed
delivery attempt; customer blames *them*, not the courier; no way to check a
parcel without calling.
**Media.** Facebook seller groups (constant), TikTok 8–11 PM, Viber for support.

### Segment B — The Receiving Customer
**Demographics.** 18–40, urban and peri-urban, buys online 2–6 times a month.
**Psychographics.** Assumes the delivery window is fiction. Plans their day
around a call that may not come.
**Pain points.** No real-time location; "out for delivery" for two days; rider
calls once and marks it failed; no compensation path when something breaks.
**Media.** Facebook, TikTok, Viber. Reads reviews before paying in advance.

### Segment C — The Corporate Shipper (highest value)
**Demographics.** 30–50, operations or supply-chain manager at a distributor,
pharma company, FMCG brand or manufacturer. Contracts worth MMK 30–400M a year.
**Psychographics.** Buys on documentation and reliability, not price. Needs to
show their own board a service-level record.
**Pain points.** No SLA reporting; damage claims unresolved for months; no cold
chain visibility; cannot audit a partner they cannot see.
**Media.** Email, LinkedIn, industry associations, direct relationships.

## 3. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

| Who | What they claim | The gap |
|---|---|---|
| Large national couriers | "Fastest nationwide" | No published performance; trust is assumed, not proven |
| App-based delivery startups | Tech-forward, young brand | Thin coverage outside Yangon; unstable rider supply |
| Traditional freight / bus cargo | Cheap, familiar | No tracking, no accountability, no documentation |
| In-house merchant fleets | Full control | Cannot scale; expensive per parcel |

Nobody publishes their on-time rate. Nobody. The first operator to do it — and
survive the bad months — takes "trustworthy" out of the category permanently.

## 4. CAMPAIGN STRATEGY

### The big idea — "The Promise You Can Track" / "ခြေရာခံနိုင်တဲ့ ကတိ"

Two meanings, both true. You can track the parcel — and you can track whether we
kept our word, because we publish the number every month.

The campaign puts a **public performance board** at the centre: on-time rate,
average delivery time by route, COD remittance speed, damage rate, claims
resolved. Updated monthly. Good months and bad months.

**Why this and not the obvious one.** The obvious campaign is a rider smiling on
a motorbike with "Fast · Safe · Reliable" over it. Every competitor already runs
it, which is exactly why nobody believes it. A published number cannot be
claimed by a competitor who is not willing to publish theirs — and the ones who
are losing will not publish. The idea defends itself.

**Brand positioning.** The courier that tells you the truth before you ask.

**Key messages.**
- "We publish our score." — the monthly board, misses included.
- "Your money, the next morning." — COD remittance as a named, measured promise.
- "Two attempts, both photographed." — proof of attempt, ending the blame argument.
- "You can see it. So can your customer." — tracking the seller can forward.

**Tone of voice.** Direct, unglamorous, accountable. Burmese written first in
everyday register — this audience is not corporate. No superlatives; the numbers
do that work.

**Hashtags.** Primary `#ခြေရာခံနိုင်တဲ့ကတိ` · Secondary `#PromiseYouCanTrack` `#ZeroExcuseDelivery`

## 9. ACTIVATION — THE SWITCH DESK

Six **Switch Desks** across 14 weeks: a two-person stand in high-density seller
markets (Mingalar Zay, Yuzana Plaza, Mandalay Zaycho) where a merchant can open
an account in eleven minutes, ship their first parcel free, and watch it move on
the tracking screen before they walk away.

Plus one **Open Hub Night** for Segment C: corporate shippers walk the sorting
hub at 10 PM, when it is busiest and least presentable. That is the point.

## 13. ROI PROJECTION

Average merchant: 45 parcels/month at MMK 2,600 contribution = MMK 117,000/month.
Retention after 12 months: 58%.

| Scenario | Merchants | Active at 12mo | Year-1 contribution (MMK) | Return on 113M |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 2,600 | 1,300 | 1,825,000,000 | 16× |
| Base | 4,000 | 2,320 | 3,257,000,000 | 29× |
| Upside | 5,200 | 3,120 | 4,380,000,000 | 39× |

Logistics returns look extreme because the unit economics are recurring — a
merchant acquired once ships every month. The number to watch is not
acquisition, it is **activation and retention**, which is exactly what the honesty
strategy is built to protect. An acquired merchant who does not trust the
promise churns in month two and the ROI collapses.
