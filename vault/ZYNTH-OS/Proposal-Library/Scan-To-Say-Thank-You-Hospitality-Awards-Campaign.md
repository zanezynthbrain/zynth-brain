## 1. CAMPAIGN OVERVIEW

Myanmar hospitality awards are decided by juries. A panel meets, a trophy is
handed over at a ballroom dinner, the winner posts a photo, and no guest who
ever stayed anywhere was asked anything.

The result is an industry honour that the industry itself quietly discounts —
and, more importantly, a housekeeper who has made four thousand beds correctly
and has never once been told so by name.

**Objective.** Establish a guest-nominated service awards programme that
collects **12,000 nominations** across 40 partner properties in one season,
converts **1,800 of those guests into referrers**, and gives the operator a
recognition asset it owns outright — over 14 weeks, on a lean pilot budget.

**Period.** 14 weeks, aligned to the festival gifting season.
**Budget envelope.** MMK 38,000,000.

**The bet.** A guest will not fill in a feedback form. A guest will spend eleven
seconds to thank a specific person by name — if you make it that easy, and if
they believe it will actually reach them.

## 2. TARGET AUDIENCE PROFILE

### Segment A — The Domestic Leisure Guest (volume)
**Demographics.** 26–48, travelling with family or friends, 2–5 domestic trips a
year, MMK 1.2M+ monthly household income.
**Location.** Originating Yangon and Mandalay; staying Bagan, Ngapali, Inle,
Pyin Oo Lwin.
**Psychographics.** Notices service intensely and says nothing. Gratitude in
Myanmar culture is expressed in person or not at all, and there is rarely a
moment to do it before checkout.
**Behaviours.** Photographs everything. Posts the trip afterwards, not during.
Asks friends for recommendations before booking, and gives them freely after.
**Pain points.** No way to thank someone specific after leaving; feedback forms
feel like complaints channels; tipping is awkward and often intercepted;
"excellent service" reviews name the hotel, never the person.
**Media.** Facebook 8–11 PM, TikTok, Viber trip-planning groups, Google reviews.

### Segment B — The MICE / Corporate Organiser (value)
**Demographics.** 30–48, arranges 4–20 corporate stays, conferences or retreats
a year. Controls MMK 40–500M of annual room-nights.
**Psychographics.** Judged by their colleagues on whether the venue worked.
Rebooks the property where a named person solved a problem for them.
**Pain points.** No continuity of contact between events; the person who saved
the last conference has been rotated; nothing distinguishes properties on
service in an RFP.
**Media.** Email, Viber, LinkedIn, direct relationships, industry associations.

### Segment C — The Hospitality Worker (the point of the programme)
**Demographics.** 19–45, housekeeping, F&B service, front office, drivers,
kitchen. Median tenure under two years.
**Psychographics.** Trained, observed, rarely named. Turnover is driven as much
by invisibility as by wage.
**Pain points.** Recognition is arbitrary and manager-mediated; guest praise
never arrives; no portable record of being good at the job.
**Why they are the campaign, not the audience.** If the nominations do not
visibly reach them, this is a data-collection exercise wearing a ribbon.

### Segment D — The Partner Property (the distribution)
**Demographics.** 40 hotels, resorts and restaurants, independent and small-group.
**Pain points.** Cannot afford their own recognition programme; lose staff to
Bangkok and Singapore; no service differentiator in a price-led market.

Nobody has built a guest-nominated, person-level recognition system in this

competitor must first persuade forty properties to share the platform.

## 4. CAMPAIGN STRATEGY

### The big idea — "Scan to Say Thank You" / "ကျေးဇူးတင်ကြောင်း စကင်န်ဖတ်ပါ"

A small card in the room, on the table, at the desk. One QR code. Scan it and a
single screen appears: **who helped you, and what did they do?**

Two fields. Eleven seconds. No account, no rating scale, no star system, no
review of the property. The nomination goes to the named person — printed,
handed over by their manager that week, and counted toward a season award
decided entirely by guests.

**Why this and not the obvious one.** The obvious hospitality campaign is a
drone over a pool, a smiling couple at sunrise, and "Experience Myanmar". It is
what every property runs, it competes on price the moment the guest compares,
and it is invisible to the person who actually delivers the experience. This
campaign takes the one thing guests genuinely feel and have no way to express,
and removes the friction from it — which is the territory the brief assigned and
the honest reading of the category.

**Brand positioning.** The programme that finally tells the person who did it.

**Key messages.**
- "Who helped you today?" — the entire ask, in one question.
- "They will see your words." — the promise that makes it worth doing.
- "Eleven seconds. No account. No stars." — the friction removed, out loud.
- "Nominated by guests, not by a panel." — the credibility claim.

**Tone of voice.** Warm, brief, unceremonious. **Burmese written first**,
English transcreated for inbound guests. No luxury-hospitality register, no
"unparalleled". The nomination screen must read like a friend asking, not a
brand surveying.

**Hashtags.** Primary `#ကျေးဇူးတင်ပါတယ်` · Secondary `#ScanToSayThankYou` `#WhoHelpedYou`

### The gifting-season fit — and its honest limit

The season is the reason the campaign lands, not decoration. Domestic travel
peaks, gratitude is culturally foregrounded, and giving is already on people's
minds. **The seasonal hook is used for timing and framing only** — it does not
require any festival tie-in, sponsorship or religious content, and the mechanic
works identically outside the season. If the operator wants to run it in June
instead, nothing breaks.

## 9. ACTIVATION — THE HANDOVER

The activation is not an event. It is **the delivery**.

Every nomination is printed within seven days and handed over by a manager, in
person, in front of colleagues. That is the moment the campaign exists for, and
it is the moment that gets filmed. Cost per handover is a printed card.

Season close: **one modest awards evening**, 120 people, at a partner property
rather than a ballroom. Winners are decided purely by guest nomination counts —
no jury, no category committee, no sponsor influence. The trophy is small. The
printed nominations are what the winners take home.

## 13. ROI PROJECTION

Referral value: a referred domestic booking averages MMK 340,000 at 38%
contribution. MICE referral averages MMK 4,100,000 at 31%.

| Scenario | Nominations | Referrers | Domestic bookings | MICE bookings | Year-1 contribution (MMK) | Return on 95.7M |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 8,000 | 1,100 | 640 | 6 | 90,300,000 | 0.9× |
| Base | 12,000 | 1,800 | 1,050 | 11 | 149,600,000 | 1.6× |
| Upside | 16,000 | 2,600 | 1,560 | 18 | 224,300,000 | 2.3× |

**Read the conservative case honestly: it does not pay back in year one.** This
is a pilot buying two things the model above cannot price — an owned recognition
platform that runs at roughly MMK 8,000,000 per subsequent season with no rebuild,
and a staff-retention effect that partner HR data suggests is real but that we
will not monetise in a proposal before measuring it.

If the operator needs certain year-one payback, this is the wrong campaign and we
would rather say so at the pitch than at the review.

## 16. WHY ZYNTH
