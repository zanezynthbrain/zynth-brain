# You Will Understand Before You Decide — Myanmar Private Healthcare Campaign ★ full client-ready

**Sector.** Healthcare — private clinic or hospital group

**Market.** Myanmar (Yangon, Mandalay)

**Type.** Integrated trust campaign — patient confidence, first-visit conversion, retention

## 1. CAMPAIGN OVERVIEW

Private healthcare marketing in Myanmar competes on hardware. New scanner.
German equipment. Foreign-trained consultant. Every clinic runs the same
advertisement with a different machine in it.

Meanwhile the actual reason patients delay care, seek second opinions abroad, or
abandon a treatment plan is almost never the equipment. It is that **nobody
explained it to them**. They left the consultation with a prescription, a price,
and no understanding of what was decided or why.

That gap is a clinical problem before it is a marketing one — an unexplained plan
is an unfollowed plan.

**Objective.** Raise first-consultation-to-treatment-plan-acceptance by **11
points**, lift 90-day follow-up attendance by 20%, and grow qualified new-patient
enquiries by 55% — over 18 weeks.

**Period.** 18 weeks. **Budget envelope.** MMK 64,000,000.

**The bet.** In a category shouting about machines, the clinic that promises
*understanding* is the one a frightened person chooses.

---

## ⚠️ Category discipline — read before anything else

This campaign makes **no clinical outcome claims**. No cure rates, no success
percentages, no comparative efficacy, no "best hospital", no patient outcome
implied or stated. Everything published is about **process** — how long we
explain, in what language, and whether the patient understood — never about
results.

Every asset passes a **medical review gate** before publication, signed by the
client's medical director. Patient stories require written informed consent in
Burmese, are anonymised by default, and may be withdrawn at any time including
after publication. No condition is named without explicit consent. No child
appears without guardian consent recorded on video.

If ZYNTH and the medical director disagree on whether an asset is safe to
publish, **the medical director's decision is final and the asset is dropped.**

## 2. TARGET AUDIENCE PROFILE

### Segment A — The Family Decision-Maker (primary)
**Demographics.** 30–52, usually the adult child or spouse arranging care for a
parent or partner. Household income MMK 1.5M+/month.
**Location.** Yangon (Bahan, Kamayut, Insein, South Okkalapa), Mandalay.
**Psychographics.** Carries the decision and the guilt. Terrified of choosing
wrong. Will spend more for confidence, not for comfort.
**Behaviours.** Asks family and colleagues first. Searches Facebook for the
clinic name plus complaints. Compares two or three, then commits fully.
**Pain points.** Consultations that last six minutes; medical Burmese they cannot
follow; a price quoted with no explanation of what is included; no idea what
happens if it does not work; the pull to send the patient to Bangkok instead.
**Media.** Facebook (all day, especially 8–11 PM), Viber family groups, YouTube
for explainers, direct phone.

### Segment B — The Chronic-Condition Patient
**Demographics.** 45–70, managing diabetes, hypertension, kidney or cardiac
conditions. Returns or should return every 1–3 months.
**Psychographics.** Has drifted off the plan before. Stops attending when the
reason for attending stops being clear.
**Pain points.** Different doctor each visit; instructions that change without
explanation; cost accumulating with no visible progress; embarrassment about
asking again.
**Media.** Facebook, Viber, family relay, community networks.

### Segment C — The Young Urban Preventive Patient
**Demographics.** 25–38, professionals, first-time private-healthcare buyers.
**Psychographics.** Health-literate, price-sensitive, sceptical of upselling.
**Pain points.** Suspects unnecessary tests; no transparent pricing; no idea what
a check-up should actually include.
**Media.** Facebook, TikTok, Google search, workplace referral.

### Segment D — Clinical Staff (internal, non-negotiable)
Doctors and nurses are the campaign's delivery mechanism. A promise of
explanation that the consultation cannot honour is worse than no campaign at
all — it converts a marketing claim into a broken one, in person.

## 4. CAMPAIGN STRATEGY

### The big idea — "You Will Understand Before You Decide" / "ဆုံးဖြတ်မယ့်အရင် နားလည်ပါစေ"

A promise about the consultation, not the outcome.

Every patient leaves with three things: the plan **written in plain Burmese**, the
cost **itemised before consent**, and — the part that changes everything — the
doctor asks the patient to **explain the plan back in their own words** before the
consultation ends. If they cannot, the doctor explains again. That is the standard.

We publish the one number that measures it: **average explanation time per
consultation**, by department, monthly. Not outcomes. Not success rates. Minutes
spent making sure someone understood.

**Why this and not the obvious one.** The obvious campaign is a scanner, a
smiling consultant, and "Advanced Care You Can Trust". It is what every
competitor runs, it is unverifiable, and it speaks to the machine rather than the
person deciding. A competitor cannot copy "understanding" without lengthening
their consultations — which costs them throughput, which is exactly why they will
not.

**Brand positioning.** The clinic that will not let you agree to something you do
not understand.

**Key messages.**
- "Say it back to me." — the teach-back, named as the standard.
- "The price before the consent." — itemised, in writing, in advance.
- "In Burmese, on paper, to take home." — the plan leaves with the patient.
- "Ask again. And again." — repeated questions framed as correct behaviour.

**Tone of voice.** Calm, plain, unhurried. **Burmese written first** — for this
campaign that is the argument, not a house rule. No white coats striding down
corridors, no dramatic music, no fear framing. Never imply that delay is the
patient's fault.

**Hashtags.** Primary `#နားလည်ပြီးမှဆုံးဖြတ်ပါ` · Secondary `#SayItBackToMe`

### The internal move that makes it real

Before any spend: teach-back training for consulting staff, the plain-Burmese
plan template built and tested with real patients, itemised pricing implemented,
and explanation time measured for two weeks to establish a baseline. **If the
consultation cannot deliver the promise, the campaign does not launch.**

## 9. ACTIVATION — THE OPEN CONSULTATION HOUR

Twice monthly, at each site, a free **Open Consultation Hour**: no treatment, no
sales, no diagnosis. A doctor answers questions from anyone who walks in —
including "should I even be here" and "is this test necessary".

Nine sessions across the campaign. Deliberately non-converting by design; it is
the most literal possible demonstration of the promise, and it is where Segment A
decides.

Plus one **Family Room** session per month for Segment B: patients bring the family
member who actually manages their condition, and the plan is explained to both.
