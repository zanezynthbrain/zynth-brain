# 26 — Myanmar Ad Craft (မြန်မာကြော်ငြာ အရေးအသား)

> How Myanmar advertising actually sounds — the reference every ZYNTH agent
> reads before writing a word of Burmese. The test is simple: would a Yangon
> reader assume a Myanmar person wrote this, or would they smell a translation?

## The one rule that matters

**Write Burmese first.** Not English-then-translate. A caption translated from
English carries English sentence shape, English rhythm and English politeness —
Myanmar readers feel it immediately even when every word is correct. Draft the
Burmese as the original, then transcreate to English.

Signs a caption was translated (all must be fixed):
- `သင်` used the way English uses "you", several times per post. Real Myanmar
  ads mostly **drop the pronoun** or use ခင်ဗျာ/ရှင် sparingly at the end.
- Long single sentences with subordinate clauses. Myanmar ad copy breaks into
  short units, often 4–7 syllables, and lets particles carry the join.
- `ဖြစ်ပါသည်` / `ဖြစ်ပါတယ်` closing every line — flattens into a report.
- Literal idiom: "level up", "game changer", "unlock" translated word for word.
- English number formats and dates left in Latin digits in a Burmese sentence.

## Register: pick one and hold it

| Register | Sounds like | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **ရေးသားစကား (formal written)** | Bank notice, government, corporate | Banks, insurance, B2B, official announcements |
| **ပြောစကား (spoken/colloquial)** | A friend telling you something useful | F&B, retail, lifestyle, most Facebook content |
| **ကြော်ငြာသံ (ad voice)** | Radio/TV spot, rhythmic, confident | Launches, promos, hooks, jingle-adjacent lines |

ZYNTH's own brand runs **spoken register with formal precision** — friendly
sentence shape, exact numbers. Client brands take whichever their audience
speaks: an SME owner reads ပြောစကား; a bank's board reads ရေးသားစကား.

## Rhythm — the thing that makes Myanmar ads stick

Myanmar advertising leans on **syllable rhythm**, not rhyme-for-rhyme. The
strongest lines land in balanced units, usually 4+4 or 5+5 syllables:

- ရိုးသားတယ် · ရှင်းလင်းတယ် (4+4)
- မြန်တယ် · သက်သာတယ် · စိတ်ချရတယ် (3-part build, common in telecom/FMCG)
- တစ်ခေါက်စားရင် · မမေ့နိုင်ဘူး (5+5)

Read every headline aloud. If it can't be said in one breath with an even beat,
it isn't a Myanmar ad line yet — it's a sentence.

**Alliteration and repeated onsets** (မြန် / မှန် / များ) are used heavily and
read as craft, not gimmick. **Rhyming end-particles** (တယ်/တယ်, ပါ/ပါ) tie two
halves together.

## Sentence-final particles carry the tone

These are not decoration — they are the tone control:

| Particle | Effect | Example use |
|---|---|---|
| `ပါ` | polite, softening | Standard for CTAs: မေးမြန်းနိုင်ပါတယ် |
| `ပဲ` | emphasis, "only/just" | ဒီတစ်ပတ်ပဲ — scarcity without shouting |
| `နော်` | warm, familiar, nudging | မမေ့နဲ့နော် — friendly reminder, not corporate |
| `တော့` | shift, "now/finally" | ရောက်လာပြီတော့ |
| `ဗျာ / ရှင်` | direct address (m/f speaker) | Use once, at most — overuse reads as sales patter |
| `လေ` | soft explanation | Good in comment replies, weak in headlines |

Over-particling is the most common failure of AI-written Burmese. **One tone
particle per sentence, maximum.**

## Numbers, money and dates

- Use **Myanmar numerals (၁၂၃)** in body copy for a Myanmar-first brand;
  Latin digits are acceptable and common for prices and phone numbers.
  Pick one convention per brand and never mix inside a single line.
- Money is spoken in **သိန်း (lakh, 100,000)** and **သန်း (million)**, not raw
  digits: `၃၈ သိန်း` reads naturally; `၃,၈၀၀,၀၀၀ ကျပ်` reads like a form.
- Percentages and discounts stay in Latin digits: `၅၀% OFF` or `50% လျှော့`.
- Dates: `စက်တင်ဘာ ၃၀` — month name in Burmese, day in numerals.

## Code-switching is normal — do it the way Yangon does

English loanwords are part of everyday Myanmar business speech. Keep the ones
people actually say; translate the ones nobody says:

**Keep in English:** post, page, inbox, delivery, promotion, discount, brand,
marketing, budget, order, booking, event, design, video, feedback, customer.

**Never leave in English:** abstract marketing nouns (engagement, awareness,
funnel, insight, alignment, ecosystem). Either express the idea in Burmese or
cut the sentence.

`Inbox ပို့လိုက်ပါ` is natural. `Brand awareness တိုးမြှင့်ပါ` is agency jargon
nobody says out loud.

## Hooks that work on Myanmar Facebook

The first line is read in the feed before "See more" cuts it. Roughly 60–80
Burmese characters. Patterns that earn the tap:

1. **Direct problem** — ကြော်ငြာပိုက်ဆံကုန်တယ်၊ ဒါပေမယ့် အရောင်းမတက်ဘူးလား။
2. **Number up front** — ၃ ရက်နဲ့ ဘာတွေလုပ်လို့ရလဲ ကြည့်လိုက်ပါ။
3. **Correction of a common belief** — Facebook မှာ လူတိုင်းရှိတယ် ဆိုတာ မဟုတ်သေးပါဘူး။
4. **Deadline / season** — သီတင်းကျွတ်အထိ ၆ ပတ်ပဲ ကျန်တော့တယ်။
5. **Insider knowledge** — အေဂျင်စီတွေ မပြောတတ်တဲ့ အချက်တစ်ခု။
6. **Question the reader already asks** — ဒီလို campaign တစ်ခု ဘယ်လောက်ကျလဲ။

Avoid opening with the brand name — Myanmar readers scroll past self-introduction
exactly like everyone else.

## Calls to action people actually follow

Strong: `Inbox ပို့လိုက်ပါ` · `ဖုန်းဆက်ပါ` · `မှတ်ထားပါ` (save) ·
`ဝေမျှပေးပါ` (share) · `Comment မှာ ရေးခဲ့ပါ` · `Message ပို့ပြီး မေးလို့ရပါတယ်`

Weak/dead: `ပိုမိုသိရှိလိုပါက ဆက်သွယ်ပါ` (formal, feels like a notice board),
`ယခုပဲ အမြန်ဆက်သွယ်ပါ` (pressure tone, reads as scam-adjacent to younger buyers).

**One CTA per post.** Two CTAs in Burmese reads as indecision, same as English.

## Festival and seasonal idiom (get these right or don't touch them)

| Season | Tone that fits | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **သင်္ကြန် (Thingyan, April)** | Joy, water, renewal, family reunion, forgiveness | Alcohol-forward imagery, mocking religious observance |
| **ဝါတွင်း (Buddhist Lent, ~Jul–Oct)** | Restraint, merit, study, family; CSR lands well | Wedding/party themes, loud celebration, discount frenzy |
| **သီတင်းကျွတ် (Thadingyut, Oct)** | Light, gratitude, respect to elders and teachers | Using candles/pagodas as pure decoration |
| **တန်ဆောင်တိုင် (Tazaungdaing, Nov)** | Lights, robe-weaving, community, night festivity | Fire imagery played for spectacle only |
| **နှစ်သစ်ကူး (New Year, Jan)** | Fresh start, planning, goals | Western party imagery with no local anchor |

**Absolute lines:** monks, pagodas, Buddha images and national symbols are never
props, never decorative background, never in a discount frame. Political content
stays out entirely. When a brand insists, escalate to the MD rather than write it.

## What Myanmar readers punish

- **Fake urgency** — "နောက်ဆုံး ၃ ခုပဲ ကျန်တော့တယ်" used every week kills trust fast.
- **Overclaiming** — "အကောင်းဆုံး", "နံပါတ်တစ်" without proof; Myanmar consumers
  are heavily scam-aware after years of online selling fraud.
- **Machine translation** — instantly visible, instantly read as a foreign
  seller or a bot.
- **Beauty/skin claims** — regulated and socially sensitive; never promise
  whitening, curing, or medical outcomes.
- **Wall-of-text captions** — break every 1–2 sentences with a line space.

## Font and typesetting notes for designed assets

- **Pyidaungsu** or **Noto Sans Myanmar** only. Never fake Myanmar with a Latin
  face, and never use Zawgyi encoding — it renders as garbage on Unicode
  devices, which is now the overwhelming majority.
- Line-height **1.9 minimum** — Myanmar stacks diacritics above and below the
  baseline and will collide at Latin leading.
- Myanmar text sits **~15% larger** than the Latin equivalent to match perceived
  weight.
- **Never letter-space Myanmar.** It breaks the visual cluster of the syllable.
- Myanmar and English **never share a line** in ZYNTH layouts — they stack.
- Never generate Burmese text inside an AI image model. Script stacking breaks
  unpredictably. Generate the imagery, typeset the Burmese over it.

## Quick self-check before any Burmese caption ships

1. Was this written in Burmese first, or translated? (If translated — rewrite.)
2. Read the hook aloud: does it land in one breath with an even beat?
3. Count `သင်`. More than once? Cut them.
4. One tone particle per sentence? One CTA in the whole post?
5. Money in သိန်း/သန်း, not raw digits?
6. Any English abstract-marketing noun left in? Cut or express in Burmese.
7. Would a Yangon shop owner send this to a friend, or scroll past it?
