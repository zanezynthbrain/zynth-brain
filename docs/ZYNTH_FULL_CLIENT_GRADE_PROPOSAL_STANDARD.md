# ZYNTH Full Client-Grade Proposal Standard

## Purpose

A ZYNTH proposal is not a content outline and not a decorative pitch. It is a **decision document, creative system, delivery blueprint, and commercial agreement-in-principle**. Its purpose is to move a client from “they understand our situation” to “this is the right idea, the right plan, the right investment, and the right partner.”

This standard establishes the minimum evidence and operating detail required for any client-ready proposal. It is deliberately a **floor, not a ceiling**. The proposal agent must add sector, channel, technical, cultural, regulatory, sponsorship, event, production, video, 3D, media, or data modules wherever the client decision requires them. The framework protects quality; it must never make an idea smaller, safer, or more generic.

> **Creative freedom rule:** The agent must create and defend ambitious, original work. It may not omit operational truth, commercial logic, cultural judgement, legal/claims control, or founder approval merely because the creative direction is ambitious.

## Proposal Release States

| State | Meaning | Permitted action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Explore** | Internal opportunity or category idea | Research, ideation, internal proposal creation only |
| **Discovery** | Client/lead information is being validated | Ask questions, conduct approved research, prepare options |
| **Draft** | Complete proposal prepared for internal review | No client release, supplier booking, spend, or publication |
| **Founder Review** | Senior commercial and creative decision required | Revise, approve, or decline only |
| **Client Ready** | Founder-approved proposal is ready to send | Send only through approved client channel |
| **Awarded / Mobilising** | Client has accepted a documented scope | Begin the agreed mobilisation and approval plan |
| **Delivered / Learning** | Work is complete or in reporting cycle | Archive evidence, results, feedback, and lessons |

## Mandatory Proposal Anatomy

### 0. Proposal Control Sheet

The first page records the proposal title, client or illustrative sector, market, proposal owner, prepared date, version, release state, decision deadline, and intended client decision. It includes a one-line ask stating **who engages ZYNTH to do what, over what period, for what investment range, to move which headline business outcome**.

### 1. Executive Summary

Written last and read first, the executive summary expresses the client opportunity in their own terms, the recommended idea in two or three sentences, the implementation scope, headline investment, key assumptions, and why ZYNTH is credible. A senior decision-maker should understand the decision within one page.

### 2. Decision Context and Challenge

This section explains the actual business problem, market moment, commercial stakes, current barriers, and the decision that marketing must help the client make. It distinguishes confirmed facts, client statements, research evidence, and working assumptions. It must never invent a client problem or present a category assumption as fact.

### 3. Market, Audience, and Category Intelligence

The proposal defines the priority market, geography, audience segment, buying or participation context, cultural considerations, relevant customer journey, category conventions, and competitive opening. It identifies what is known, what must be verified, and how the plan changes between Myanmar, Singapore, or any other proposed market.

### 4. The Human Insight and Opportunity

A proposal needs one sharp, human tension that makes the idea necessary. This is not a demographic label. It identifies what the audience is trying to feel, avoid, achieve, signal, or resolve—and why the client brand can credibly help. The section ends with a concise opportunity statement that guides every later choice.

### 5. Creative Territories

ZYNTH develops **at least three distinct creative territories** before recommending one. Each territory is named, expressed in one provocative line, linked to the insight, and described through its emotional payoff, brand role, cultural relevance, channel opportunity, execution feasibility, and commercial risk. The proposal documents why the recommended territory wins and why obvious alternatives were not chosen.

### 6. Recommended Concept and Full Explanation

The selected concept receives its own complete section:

| Required element | Required explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Campaign/event/product title | Distinctive, ownable working name and optional bilingual treatment |
| Concept in one line | The clearest possible expression of the core idea |
| Concept narrative | How the idea works emotionally, socially, commercially, and visually |
| Brand role | What the brand does beyond appearing in the work |
| Signature moment | The one experience, story, interaction, or transformation people remember |
| Key messages | Core message, supporting proof, and prohibited/unverified claim boundaries |
| Visual, spatial, sound, and tone world | How the concept should look, feel, move, sound, and behave |
| Cultural and inclusion lens | How the concept respects local timing, language, community, accessibility, and sensitivity |
| Why now | Why this concept is relevant to the particular market and business moment |

### 7. Experience, Customer, or Conversion Journey

The proposal maps the journey from first awareness through participation, evaluation, conversion, retention, advocacy, or post-event follow-up. Each stage states the audience need, ZYNTH intervention, client/partner role, data/consent requirement, and intended next action.

### 8. Integrated Campaign, Event, or Service Architecture

This is the master plan made concrete. It maps owned, earned, paid, shared, retail, sales, partner, public-event, creator, CRM, web, and experiential channels according to their role. It defines phase objectives, channel choices, message adaptation, production dependencies, and sequencing.

For event work, include venue/format logic, guest journey, programme, stage and spatial design, AV/lighting/content, crew roles, run-of-show logic, permits, accessibility, safety, weather/power contingencies, and partner/sponsor integration. For digital work, include conversion paths, measurement instrumentation, media test plan, account ownership, data flow, and optimisation cadence. For video/3D work, include treatment, storyboard, technical approach, source-file plan, rights, render/output standards, and review gates.

### 9. Deliverables and Acceptance Criteria

Every deliverable is listed with description, quantity, format, language, production owner, included revisions, approval owner, delivery date, and acceptance criteria. “Content package,” “event support,” and “marketing” are not valid deliverables without a concrete definition.

### 10. Workstreams, Timeline, and Operating Rhythm

The plan contains a week-by-week or phase-by-phase implementation table. It identifies ZYNTH tasks, client tasks, partner tasks, approvals, critical path, decision deadlines, and the impact of delays. It defines the operating rhythm for creative review, commercial review, reporting, crisis escalation, and change control.

### 11. Production, Supplier, and Technology Plan

All necessary suppliers, platforms, source files, equipment, permissions, technical requirements, lead times, and data dependencies are described. A supplier table distinguishes **verified**, **indicative**, and **to source** status. Contacts and rates are never fabricated. A technology/AI plan identifies tool purpose, human review point, data/rights controls, and fallback.

### 12. Itemised Investment and Funding Plan

Budgets are built from the bottom up—not guessed from a headline total. The main table records item, workstream, quantity, unit, local currency, foreign-currency exposure, tax treatment, source status, and subtotal. It separately identifies ZYNTH professional fees, third-party direct costs, paid media, talent/creator fees, venue/production costs, platform/technology, travel, permits, insurance, and contingency.

Myanmar proposals state MMK terms, USD/SGD exposure where relevant, rate date/source, and a currency-review clause for longer work. Singapore proposals state SGD terms and applicable tax assumptions. The proposal must show a conservative, expected, and stretch scope where uncertainty materially changes cost.

### 13. ZYNTH Commercial Model and Payment Terms

This section explains management fee, production margin, service fee, or retainer model; contribution-margin logic; exclusions; deposit/payment milestones; revision/change-order policy; cancellation; IP/usage rights; client responsibilities; confidentiality; and payment conditions. Sponsorship, ticketing, or hybrid funding models require a separate commercial architecture, rights inventory, fulfilment plan, and sponsor-return logic.

### 14. Results, Value Case, and Measurement Plan

The proposal names the expected outcomes, the assumptions required, baseline/evidence availability, KPI definitions, tool/data source, reporting cadence, decision thresholds, and action when the plan is underperforming. It separates leading indicators from business outcomes. It must not promise ROI, sales, or reach without a traceable basis and client-controlled dependencies.

### 15. Risk, Governance, and Contingency

A risk register covers commercial, creative, cultural, claims, legal, data/privacy, platform, talent, production, weather, safety, accessibility, political/operational, currency, reputational, and dependency risks as relevant. Each row records likelihood, impact, owner, mitigation, trigger, and fallback. The proposal names the founder and client approval gates required before spend, public release, vendor commitment, data collection, or production.

### 16. Why ZYNTH

This section is specific and evidence-based. It explains the relevant ZYNTH method, cross-market insight, senior attention, creative/production capability, AI-enabled speed under human control, and proof that can genuinely be substantiated. It never invents case studies, awards, clients, vendor contacts, or performance results.

### 17. Terms, Next Step, and Mobilisation Plan

The proposal ends with one clear action: discovery approval, scope approval, a workshop, a budget decision, a pilot, or contract mobilisation. It states what happens in the first ten business days after approval, what information or access ZYNTH needs, the decision owner, and the deadline that protects the intended launch date.

## Specialist Modules to Add When Relevant

| Situation | Required additional module |
| --- | --- |
| Product launch | Product truth, offer, retail/ecommerce readiness, inventory, launch sequence, sales enablement |
| TikTok/creator campaign | Creator selection, audience audit, disclosure, usage rights, moderation, content amplification, safety |
| Sponsorship | Rights inventory, sponsor fit, valuation, activation, fulfilment, proof/reporting, no-conflict rules |
| Public event | Venue feasibility, permits, crowd/safety, accessibility, run-of-show, RACI, weather/power plans |
| Corporate event | Executive outcomes, guest profile, protocol, show flow, stakeholder management, post-event follow-up |
| Video production | Treatment, storyboard, cast/talent, shoot plan, rights, edit/masters, platform cutdowns, audio/subtitles |
| 3D or spatial design | Site/measurement assumptions, floor plan, materials, 3D renders, source files, safety/build feasibility, renders and handover |
| Paid media | Channel rationale, campaign structure, tracking, media budget, optimisation rule, account ownership |
| Regulated sector | Claims review, legal/compliance approval, disclosure, sensitive data, escalation matrix |
| Myanmar/Singapore dual-market | Market differences, language/localisation, FX, local suppliers, country-specific approval/operational plan |

## Quality Gate Before Founder Review

The proposal agent and human reviewer score each proposal from 1 to 10 across client understanding, market grounding, insight quality, originality, execution reality, financial accuracy, value clarity, measurement, risk management, cultural appropriateness, language/polish, and decision readiness. Nothing below 8 is released. A score must be supported by the actual document—not a self-congratulatory statement.

## Adaptive Intelligence Rule

The agents must treat this standard as a **scaffold for completeness**, not a box that limits capability. They may propose new service models, creative formats, technologies, collaborations, market entries, sponsorship structures, experiences, cultural moments, audience communities, and breakthrough ideas when they can explain the rationale, resource requirements, risk, and approval path. Internal exploration remains broad; client release remains accountable.
