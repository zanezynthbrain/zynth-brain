# ZYNTH Business Development Playbook

## Commercial Thesis

ZYNTH wins when it is selected as the agency that makes difficult growth, launch, culture, experience, and market-entry decisions clearer—and then can deliver the complete creative and operating system behind the decision. The business-development process must prove that value before it asks for a large mandate. It should not rely on generic “we are a full-service agency” outreach or on giving away the final proposal before the client has committed to discovery.

## Ideal Client Profile

| Tier | Profile | ZYNTH offer to lead with | Qualification test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tier A: Strategic account** | Organisation with a clear Myanmar or Singapore growth, launch, brand, experience, or regional challenge | Clarity Sprint, Regional Growth Bridge, Launch Room, Experience & Event Lab | Material decision, realistic budget, accessible senior owner, potential for repeat/portfolio work |
| **Tier B: Growth account** | SME or scale-up with a credible offer but fragmented marketing and decision process | Growth Engine, Brand Foundation, Social Culture Studio | Evidence of demand, willingness to measure, operational capacity to act |
| **Tier C: Production opportunity** | Brand/team that needs a specific creative, video, event, 3D, or social asset | Content and Motion Studio | Clear brief, rights, budget, timetable, decision owner; can lead to strategic work |
| **Disqualify or nurture** | No decision owner, no solvable problem, no budget reality, inappropriate ethics/risk, or expectation of unpaid complete strategy | A short value resource or future follow-up | Do not over-service; protect team capacity and IP |

## Account Intelligence Card

Before outreach, the agent prepares a one-page Account Intelligence Card. It includes organisation and sector, market, named decision roles only where publicly/legitimately known, current offers, visible marketing/experience signals, customer/audience hypothesis, strategic trigger, likely business challenge, competitor context, ZYNTH relevance, evidence URLs, unknowns, risk/sensitivity, proposed first value, and recommended next action.

No agent may invent a contact, buying trigger, budget, or business issue. The founder approves external use of account-specific analysis and all initial outreach.

## New-Business Pipeline

| Stage | Definition | Evidence needed | Exit decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Radar | Potential account/sector signal noticed | Source and initial relevance | Research or archive |
| Qualified | Commercial fit and a plausible need identified | Account card, timing hypothesis, decision route | Prepare value-first angle |
| Warmed | Account has received or engaged with relevant value | Recorded interaction/permission | Request conversation |
| Discovery | A real conversation or approved brief exists | Client inputs, decision owner, problem statement | Offer paid Clarity Sprint or scoped proposal |
| Proposal | Full client-grade proposal in Founder Review or Client Ready state | Complete proposal, budget, risk, internal score | Send only when approved |

| Lost / Nurture | Not moving now | Honest reason, future trigger, next contact window | Learn and retain relationship appropriately |

## Value-First Outreach Standard

The first contact is not a brochure. It is an observation that proves preparation and earns a conversation. It should identify a relevant market moment or customer tension, avoid false certainty, offer a short practical insight or question, and request a modest next step. It must never offer a fully developed concept, client-specific unpaid strategy, unsupported claim, or unsolicited high-pressure pitch.

### Outreach Sequence

1. **Observe:** identify a public trigger, category moment, customer tension, or material launch/event signal.
2. **Make it useful:** prepare a concise account-relevant insight, sector note, or question.
3. **Ask permission:** request a 20–30 minute discovery conversation, not a project commitment.
4. **Diagnose:** learn the decision, economic stakes, audience, timeline, internal capability, budget approach, and approval path.
5. **Sell clarity:** recommend a paid Clarity Sprint when scope remains uncertain.
6. **Propose completely:** prepare a full ZYNTH proposal only after discovery or a genuinely confirmed brief.
7. **Follow through:** record action, owner, due date, and learning after every interaction.

## Proposal Conversion Process

The business-development lead owns the relationship; the proposal team owns the quality of the decision document. The proposal is created by a roundtable of market, strategy, creative, commercial, delivery, and risk perspectives. The proposal team must develop three territories, pressure-test the recommended concept, build the cost from bottom up, and name the assumptions preventing certainty.

Before release, the founder confirms that the proposal is suitable for the actual account, commercial terms protect ZYNTH, the creative is strong enough to win, supplier or capability claims are accurate, the risk is tolerable, and the proposed next step is specific.

## Weekly Founder Business-Development Review

The weekly review lasts 45–60 minutes and answers five questions:

| Question | Evidence required | Founder decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where is the best new revenue opportunity? | Qualified pipeline by value, probability, timing, and fit | Which opportunities receive senior attention? |
| What is blocking conversion? | Stalled stage, objection pattern, missing proof, price/scope issue | Remove obstacle, change offer, or stop pursuit |
| What work is at risk? | Project/relationship/margin/scope risk register | Escalate, renegotiate, or protect delivery |
| Is ZYNTH learning? | Win/loss data, proposal score, performance/case evidence | Improve a package, skill, or proof asset |
| What should the founder do personally? | Three highest-leverage introductions, decisions, or meetings | Commit an owner and deadline |

## Core Metrics

ZYNTH tracks qualified account count, discovery conversations, paid discovery conversion, proposal volume, proposal-to-win rate, sales cycle, average professional fee, project/retainer value, contribution margin, collection days, renewal/expansion, sector mix, source of opportunity, win/loss reason, founder time spent, and proof asset usage. Metrics are useful only when they change action.

## Partnership and Referral Model

Build a partner map of complementary capability, not a vague contact list: production, media, data/technology, venues, technical/AV, events, creators/talent, research, PR, legal/compliance, and Singapore/Myanmar execution partners. Every partner is assessed for capability, reliability, price basis, lead time, rights, safety, conflict, cultural/market fit, and commercial terms. Referral or partner agreements require founder approval and documented client transparency where appropriate.

## Ninety-Day Targets

The first 90 days should focus on building a quality pipeline, not impressive raw lead volume. A reasonable internal target is 40 thoroughly qualified account cards, 12 senior discovery conversations, 4 paid clarity/strategy engagements, 3 full client-grade proposals, and 1–2 won engagements. These are operating targets, not promises; they must be adjusted after the first monthly evidence review.

## Founder-Only Boundaries

The founder retains final authority for client outreach policy, public portfolio/case use, discounted commercial terms, new service promises, major proposals, payments/credit, partner commitments, public events, sensitive sectors, publisher/creator contracts, and any material spend. Agents can prepare options, alerts, drafts, schedules, account research, and decision memos continuously.
