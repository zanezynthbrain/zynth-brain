# ZYNTH Founder Operating Guide

**Owner:** Zane, Managing Director  
**System:** ZYNTH Founder Command and Internal AI Agency Workforce  
**Markets:** Myanmar (primary) and Singapore (prepared expansion)  
**Status date:** 15 August 2026  
**Live command centre:** <https://zynth-ai-marketing-firm.up.railway.app>  
**Operating repository:** <https://github.com/zanezynthbrain/zynth-brain>

> **Purpose.** This guide explains how ZYNTH’s founder-controlled agency operating system works in daily practice. The system now operates continuously on internal work, while Zane remains the recorded decision-maker for any client communication, publication, spend, vendor booking, project commitment, or production release.

## 1. The operating principle

ZYNTH is configured as an **internal AI workforce**, not an unsupervised external agency. Its role is to continuously create and improve market intelligence, proposal material, creative territories, commercial learning, system documentation, and founder-ready decision briefs. Its work is intended to increase preparedness and the quality of decisions; it is not permission to represent ZYNTH externally without founder approval.

The operating model separates two lanes. The **Explore lane** is continuous and autonomous: research, draft proposals, concept packages, creative preparation, quality review, and knowledge consolidation. The **Commitment lane** is founder-gated: prospect contact, proposal issuance to a client, publication, paid-media release, production activation, procurement, vendor booking, financial commitment, and project-stage advancement.

| Lane | The system may do this without waiting | Founder approval is required before this happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Explore** | Research a market, create a concept, write a full draft proposal, prepare a creative brief, flag risks, build a client-ready asset plan, learn from internal outcomes, refresh internal knowledge. | Not applicable, because these outputs remain internal drafts and clearly labelled hypotheses where appropriate. |
| **Commitment** | Prepare the exact next action for review. | Contact a prospect or client; send a proposal; publish a post; commit money; book a vendor; launch paid media; route a daily concept into image/3D production; mark a lead as active, won, delivery, or done. |

## 3. The daily founder rhythm

The system should make the founder’s day clearer, not noisier. Use the weekday brief as the decision point, then inspect only the material that can move a priority forward.

| Moment | Founder action | Expected output from the system |
|---|---|---|
| **08:30 Myanmar / 10:00 Singapore, weekdays** | Read the scheduled Founder Brief and choose the one to three decisions worth making that day. | Strategic priorities, market signals, items awaiting review, risks, learning prompt, and recommended next actions. |
| **During the day** | Open **Today** in the command centre. Review any decision card, internal concept, or project health signal that requires direction. | Current confirmed records, internal concepts, pipeline indicators, and controlled queues. |
| **When a concept is promising** | Press **Review**, give a direction, or create/approve a linked founder project. | A stronger proposal, package, storyboard, event plan, campaign concept, or production-ready brief. |
| **Before any external step** | Record a founder approval in the project/approval flow. | A traceable authority record; only then can relevant production or client-facing preparation progress. |
| **Weekly** | Review the internal weekly report and decide which package, vertical, and account list deserve commercial attention. | Portfolio review, learning review, research changes, and proposed next priorities. |

The existing weekday Founder Brief schedule is active at **10:00 SGT / 08:30 Myanmar time, Monday through Friday**. It is a separate scheduled founder-assistant cadence and should be treated as the primary briefing ritual.

## 5. Reviewing the three daily concept packages

Every daily package should be treated as a **creative territory with commercial logic**, not as a finished client promise. The workforce covers multiple sectors and formats over time, including campaigns, launches, events, sponsorships, omnichannel/digital work, social, TikTok challenges, corporate events, stage concepts, and video-storyboard directions.

A useful founder review takes five minutes per concept. First, ask whether the situation is real and timely. Second, check whether the human insight, creative device, and business goal fit together. Third, identify the right ZYNTH service package and a realistic buyer. Fourth, ask for evidence gaps or feasibility risks to be labelled. Finally, decide one of three directions: archive, refine, or attach to an approved founder project.

| Review question | A good answer looks like | If the answer is weak |
|---|---|---|
| **Why now?** | A credible market, festival, category, seasonal, or business trigger. | Ask for evidence or switch the territory. |
| **Why would people care?** | A specific human or professional tension, not a generic marketing claim. | Ask for a stronger insight and alternative territory. |
| **Why is it commercially useful?** | A clear objective, buyer, measurement approach, and service-package logic. | Request a commercial rationale before advancing. |
| **Can ZYNTH deliver it?** | Feasibility, partners, dependencies, timing, and budget assumptions are explicit. | Mark dependencies as TBC and scope a leaner option. |
| **What is the next controlled step?** | An internal proposal improvement, a founder project, or an approval request. | Do not route it to production or external communication. |

## 6. The full proposal standard

A proposal is no longer a one-page idea. The master proposal capability follows the full client-grade standard in [`ZYNTH_FULL_CLIENT_GRADE_PROPOSAL_STANDARD.md`](ZYNTH_FULL_CLIENT_GRADE_PROPOSAL_STANDARD.md). A strong proposal integrates the following components in one coherent commercial story.

| Required component | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Campaign or event title, executive summary, context, problem, opportunity, goals, and success definition | Establishes a decision-ready strategic frame. |
| Concept explanation, creative territories, audience insight, messaging, journey, and channel logic | Makes the idea understandable and defensible. |
| Scope, setup, production, event, content, media, vendor, governance, and delivery detail | Shows how the proposal becomes real work rather than a mood board. |
| Timeline, roles, approval points, risk register, measurement, reporting, and optimisation plan | Makes responsibility, feasibility, and control visible. |
| Myanmar and Singapore budget framing, assumptions, exclusions, optional additions, and payment logic | Protects commercial clarity and prevents invented certainty. |
| Evidence, source discipline, unknowns, and explicit TBC items | Preserves information truth and avoids claiming facts that have not been verified. |

The repository includes two complete examples: a product-launch proposal and a public brand-experience proposal. They should be used as depth and completeness benchmarks, not copied blindly into client work.

## 8. How project approvals work

Agent-discovered leads cannot advance into proposal, won, delivery, or done stages without founder approval. Daily concepts also cannot reach image or 3D production unless there is a linked, founder-approved project. This keeps the most important boundary technically enforceable, not merely written as an instruction.

| Situation | Correct founder action | What must remain blocked until then |
|---|---|---|
| A research lead appears promising | Create or approve a founder project and record the decision. | Client contact, actual proposal issuance, stage advancement. |
| A daily concept deserves a visual prototype | Review it, create/link an approved project, and specify the internal/external scope. | Image or Blender/3D production routing from the daily concept. |
| A client proposal is ready to leave ZYNTH | Verify facts, budget, legal/commercial terms, scope, dates, dependencies, and the recipient; then explicitly approve sending. | Sending or presenting it as a firm client commitment. |
| A social/posting idea is ready | Approve the final material, destination, timing, and claims. | Publishing, boosting, or paid-media release. |
| A vendor or venue is proposed | Approve the budget ceiling and commitment after current quotations are verified. | Booking, purchase, deposit, or contractual agreement. |

## 9. Telegram command use

The connected Telegram bot is a companion interface for brief, controlled commands. Use it for quick status and founder review, not as a replacement for careful project approval. Core commands include `/workforce` for the internal daily workforce view, `/active` for master internal autonomy, and `/switch <name> on|off` for individual controls. The dashboard is preferable when reviewing detailed concepts, projects, and health signals.

> **Safety rule.** Never treat a bot-generated draft, recommendation, or research result as a confirmed claim or an approved client communication. The approval record, not the channel, is the authority.

The live service also currently reports that the Obsidian vault is missing because its runtime path resolves relative to the backend directory. This is a deployment-path issue, not a loss of the repository vault. The next maintenance patch should resolve the repository root reliably in the Railway layout.

## 12. The next 30 days

The goal of the next 30 days is not to generate more ideas than can be acted upon. It is to turn the best internal material into a disciplined pipeline, a credible evidence base, and a repeatable delivery rhythm. The priorities below are arranged to support realistic revenue development without promising a specific outcome.

| Window | Founder priority | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| **Days 1–7** | Review the strongest daily packages; select three priority verticals and 20 named target accounts; tighten the service-package one-pagers and proof points. | A founder-approved account list, three offer narratives, and at least three polished internal proposals. |
| **Days 8–14** | Turn selected concepts into vertical-specific pitch packs; verify pricing, case-study evidence, vendor assumptions, and procurement constraints. | A proposal library that can be customised quickly without inventing claims or costs. |
| **Days 15–21** | Choose a small number of prospects for founder-approved, personal outreach; track response quality, meetings, objections, and next actions. | A traceable pipeline with real founder-approved conversations, not vanity lead counts. |
| **Days 22–30** | Review conversion patterns; improve offers, proposal modules, and follow-up logic; decide the next industry focus from evidence. | A weekly commercial review with a clear next-month account and package strategy. |

For the founder personally, the highest-leverage behaviour is to make decisions quickly at the boundary between exploration and commitment. The system will create options and prepare evidence. Zane should use that preparation to choose fewer, better commercial bets; verify the most consequential assumptions; and create human relationships with the accounts that fit ZYNTH’s strengths.
